## PREAMBLE: EVIDENCE HIERARCHY USED IN THIS DOCUMENT

Throughout this document, claims are tagged with one of three evidence levels:
- **[PROVEN EFFICACY]** — Statistically significant improvement in a pre-specified primary endpoint in a randomized controlled trial
- **[PHARMACODYNAMIC ACTIVITY]** — Measurable biological effect (T-cell induction, TME changes, ctDNA response) without confirmed clinical benefit
- **[MECHANISTIC PLAUSIBILITY]** — Preclinical or correlative data supporting a hypothesis; not yet tested or confirmed in humans

## SYNTHESIS MEMO

### 1. THE DOMINANT FAILURE PATTERN IN MSS CRC IO: WHY BROAD ENROLLMENT CONSISTENTLY FAILS

**The core problem is not a single mechanism — it is a layered, redundant immunosuppressive architecture.**

MSS CRC (~95% of metastatic CRC) fails IO for the following reasons, operating simultaneously:

**Layer 1: Insufficient neoantigen load.** MSS CRC has a median TMB of 3-5 mutations/Mb vs. 40-50 in MSI-H. The neoantigen density is insufficient to prime a spontaneous anti-tumor T-cell response. Even when vaccines or chemotherapy generate neoantigens, the density remains below the threshold for durable immune recognition.

**Layer 2: Structural T-cell exclusion via TGF-β.** TGF-β is the dominant immune exclusion cytokine in MSS CRC. Tauriello et al. (Nature 2018) demonstrated that TGF-β drives T-cell exclusion in genetically reconstituted CRC metastasis models, and that TGF-β blockade + anti-PD-L1 caused tumor regression preclinically. Clinically, TGF-β signaling correlates with the "excluded" immune phenotype (T cells at tumor margins, not infiltrating). SMAD4 loss (20% of CRC) creates a TGF-β-independent immune exclusion mechanism via CXCL1/8-CXCR2-mediated neutrophil recruitment.

**Layer 3: Liver metastasis-induced systemic immune tolerance.** The liver is the dominant metastatic site in CRC (~70% of patients). Yu et al. (Nat Med 2021) demonstrated that liver metastases siphon activated CD8+ T cells and induce their apoptosis via FasL-expressing macrophages, creating systemic immune tolerance. This explains why the non-liver-metastatic subgroup consistently shows IO benefit (REGONIVO, RIN, CO.26) while liver-metastatic patients show near-zero response rates. This is not a tumor-intrinsic mechanism — it is a host-organ mechanism.

**Layer 4: KRAS-driven MDSC recruitment.** KRAS mutation (40% of CRC) represses IRF2, which increases CXCL3 expression, which recruits CXCR2+ MDSCs to the TME (Liao et al. Cancer Cell 2019). MDSCs suppress T-cell function through multiple mechanisms (arginase, ROS, TGF-β secretion). KRAS-mutant CRC is therefore doubly immunosuppressive.

**Layer 5: Immunoscore-low baseline.** The majority of MSS CRC tumors are "cold" (Immunoscore 0-1) with minimal CD8+ T-cell infiltration. Without pre-existing immune infiltration, checkpoint blockade has no T cells to reinvigorate.

**Why broad enrollment fails:** When all five layers are present simultaneously (the typical MSS CRC patient with liver metastases, KRAS mutation, low Immunoscore, and high TGF-β), no single IO intervention can overcome the redundant suppression. Broad enrollment mixes the ~20-30% of patients with separable biology (immune-inflamed, non-liver-metastatic, pTMB-high) with the ~70-80% who are structurally resistant, diluting any signal to statistical noise.

**The IMblaze370 lesson is paradigmatic:** MEK inhibition was mechanistically rational (upregulates MHC-I, promotes T-cell infiltration in preclinical models), but the phase III trial showed no benefit. The preclinical models did not recapitulate the full complexity of human MSS CRC TME, particularly the liver metastasis-induced systemic tolerance.

### 2. BIOMARKER OR SUBGROUP STRUCTURES SHOWING SEPARABLE SIGNAL

The following biomarker/subgroup structures have shown **statistically or clinically meaningful** separable signals in MSS CRC IO trials:

**A. Absence of liver metastases [STRONGEST CLINICAL SIGNAL]**
- Evidence: REGONIVO (ORR 33% in NLM vs. ~0% in liver-met), RIN phase I (ORR ~40% NLM vs. ~5% liver-met), CO.26 (OS HR ~0.50 NLM vs. ~0.90 liver-met), AtezoTRIBE (liver mets did NOT predict resistance when Immunoscore-IC was high — nuance)
- Mechanism: Liver immune tolerance via macrophage-mediated CD8+ T-cell elimination (Yu et al. Nat Med 2021)
- Clinical implication: Non-liver-metastatic MSS CRC (lung-predominant, peritoneal-only) is a biologically distinct subgroup with meaningful IO sensitivity
- **Evidence level: PROVEN EFFICACY in subgroup analyses; not yet validated in prospective biomarker-enriched trial**

**B. High plasma/tissue TMB (pTMB ≥28 variants/Mb) [MODERATE SIGNAL]**
- Evidence: CO.26 pTMB ≥28 subgroup OS HR 0.34 (90% CI 0.18–0.63)
- Mechanism: Higher neoantigen density enables T-cell priming; pTMB ≥28 is ~3x the standard TMB-H threshold
- Clinical implication: Standard TMB-H (≥10 mut/Mb) is insufficient for MSS CRC; a higher threshold (≥28) may identify a rare but responsive subgroup
- **Evidence level: PHARMACODYNAMIC ACTIVITY + clinical signal in subgroup; not prospectively validated**

**C. Immunoscore-IC high (CD8+/PD-L1 proximity) [MODERATE SIGNAL]**
- Evidence: AtezoTRIBE pMMR subgroup with high Immunoscore-IC: PFS HR ~0.50 (exploratory)
- Mechanism: Pre-existing immune infiltration enables checkpoint blockade to reinvigorate T cells
- Clinical implication: ~20-30% of pMMR/MSS CRC tumors are immune-inflamed and may benefit from IO
- **Evidence level: PHARMACODYNAMIC ACTIVITY + exploratory clinical signal; not prospectively validated**

**D. DetermaIO gene expression signature [EMERGING SIGNAL]**
- Evidence: AtezoTRIBE translational analysis: DetermaIO-high pMMR patients showed PFS HR ~0.45
- Mechanism: 27-gene immune-related expression signature predicting IO benefit
- **Evidence level: PHARMACODYNAMIC ACTIVITY; requires prospective validation**

**E. Low ctDNA at maintenance initiation [EMERGING SIGNAL]**
- Evidence: GRANITE low-ctDNA subgroup PFS HR 0.50 (exploratory)
- Mechanism: Low tumor burden at maintenance initiation may allow vaccine-primed T cells to achieve tumor control
- **Evidence level: PHARMACODYNAMIC ACTIVITY; exploratory; not validated**

**F. KRAS A59T variant [MECHANISTIC PLAUSIBILITY]**
- Evidence: Lou et al. Cells 2021: KRAS A59T-mutant CRC shows enriched IO biomarkers (PD-L1, TMB, immune infiltrate) compared to other KRAS variants
- **Evidence level: MECHANISTIC PLAUSIBILITY; no clinical trial data**

### 3. WHAT THE VACCINE/ANTIGEN-DIRECTED LITERATURE TEACHES ABOUT TRANSLATION GAPS

**The central lesson: Pharmacodynamic activity ≠ clinical benefit in MSS CRC.**

The vaccine literature in MSS CRC is remarkably consistent in demonstrating robust T-cell induction with zero or near-zero clinical benefit. This pattern teaches several critical lessons:

**Lesson 3a: Peripheral T-cell induction is necessary but not sufficient.**
Every vaccine trial (GVAX, PANVAC, BN-CV301, PolyPEPI1018, GRANITE, neoantigen vaccines) has demonstrated antigen-specific T-cell responses in peripheral blood. None has demonstrated that these T cells traffic to and function within MSS CRC tumors at sufficient scale to cause tumor regression. The bottleneck is not T-cell priming — it is T-cell trafficking and effector function within the TME.

**Lesson 3b: The TME is the graveyard of vaccine-primed T cells.**
Even when biopsies show increased CD8+ infiltration after vaccination (GVAX, PexaVec), the T cells are functionally suppressed by TGF-β, MDSCs, and regulatory T cells. The GVAX + pembrolizumab trial is the clearest example: T-cell responses generated, tumor infiltration increased, but ORR 0%.

**Lesson 3c: Antigen selection matters but is not the primary bottleneck.**
Shared tumor-associated antigens (CEA, MUC1) generate T-cell responses but face central tolerance issues (these are self-antigens). Personalized neoantigens (GRANITE, NeoAg-VAX) generate stronger, more specific T-cell responses but still fail to translate to clinical benefit in established metastatic MSS CRC. The antigen is not the limiting factor — the TME is.

**Lesson 3d: The minimal residual disease setting may be different.**
AMPLIFY-201 (mKRAS vaccine in ctDNA+ post-surgery patients) showed ctDNA clearance in 3/5 CRC patients. This suggests that vaccine-primed T cells may be effective against microscopic residual disease but not against established metastatic tumors with fully developed immunosuppressive TMEs. The GRANITE low-ctDNA subgroup signal supports this hypothesis.

**Lesson 3e: Combination with checkpoint blockade is necessary but not sufficient.**
All recent vaccine trials combine with PD-1/PD-L1 blockade. This is mechanistically rational (prevent exhaustion of vaccine-primed T cells) but has not been sufficient. The additional suppressive mechanisms (TGF-β, MDSCs, liver tolerance) are not addressed by PD-1 blockade alone.

**Lesson 3f: The timing of IO relative to chemotherapy matters.**
MODUL (maintenance IO after chemo induction) failed completely. AtezoTRIBE (concurrent IO with intensive chemo) showed a modest signal. This suggests that concurrent immunogenic cell death (from chemotherapy) + IO may be more effective than sequential approaches, but the window of immunogenicity is narrow.

### 4. COMBINATION STRATEGIES WITH MOST MECHANISTIC PROMISE (WITHOUT EFFICACY PROOF)

Ranked by mechanistic coherence and available supporting data:

**A. TKI (VEGFR/MET/AXL) + PD-1/PD-L1 in non-liver-metastatic MSS CRC [HIGHEST PROMISE]**
- Rationale: VEGF inhibition reduces immunosuppressive myeloid cells and promotes T-cell infiltration; MET/AXL inhibition targets immunosuppressive macrophage polarization; non-liver-metastatic selection removes the dominant resistance mechanism
- Evidence: CAMILLA (cabozantinib + durvalumab) ORR 27.6% in pMMR/MSS CRC; RIN ORR ~40% in NLM subgroup; STELLAR-303 ongoing
- **Evidence level: PHARMACODYNAMIC ACTIVITY + clinical signal in small trials; phase III ongoing**

**B. Neoantigen vaccine + checkpoint blockade in low-ctDNA / minimal residual disease setting [HIGH PROMISE]**
- Rationale: Vaccine-primed T cells may control microscopic disease; low tumor burden reduces TME suppression; ctDNA clearance as pharmacodynamic endpoint
- Evidence: GRANITE low-ctDNA subgroup HR 0.50; AMPLIFY-201 ctDNA clearance in CRC
- **Evidence level: PHARMACODYNAMIC ACTIVITY + exploratory clinical signal**

**C. TGF-β inhibition + PD-1/PD-L1 in immune-excluded MSS CRC [MODERATE PROMISE]**
- Rationale: TGF-β is the dominant T-cell exclusion mechanism; dual blockade is mechanistically rational (Tauriello et al. Nature 2018)
- Evidence: Bintrafusp alfa failed in established metastatic CRC (ORR 0%); however, patient selection was not enriched for TGF-β-high/immune-excluded phenotype
- **Evidence level: MECHANISTIC PLAUSIBILITY; clinical translation has failed in unselected patients; enriched trial not yet done**

**D. KRAS G12C inhibitor + PD-1 blockade in KRAS G12C-mutant MSS CRC [MODERATE PROMISE]**
- Rationale: KRAS G12C inhibition increases MHC-I expression, reduces MDSC recruitment, and promotes T-cell infiltration (preclinical data); KRAS G12C is ~3-4% of CRC
- Evidence: Preclinical data (Tian et al. Cancer Res 2023; Patel et al. Cancer Res 2024); no clinical efficacy data yet
- **Evidence level: MECHANISTIC PLAUSIBILITY; phase I/II trials ongoing**

**E. Intensive chemo (FOLFOXIRI) + bevacizumab + IO in Immunoscore-IC-high pMMR/MSS CRC [MODERATE PROMISE]**
- Rationale: FOLFOXIRI induces immunogenic cell death; bevacizumab reduces VEGF-mediated immunosuppression; Immunoscore-IC-high selection identifies pre-existing immune infiltration
- Evidence: AtezoTRIBE Immunoscore-IC-high subgroup PFS HR ~0.50
- **Evidence level: PHARMACODYNAMIC ACTIVITY + exploratory clinical signal; prospective enrichment trial needed**

**F. Oncolytic virus + checkpoint blockade in non-liver-metastatic MSS CRC [LOWER PROMISE]**
- Rationale: Oncolytic viruses induce immunogenic cell death, release DAMPs, and convert cold tumors to hot; combination with checkpoint blockade may sustain T-cell responses
- Evidence: PexaVec + durvalumab ± tremelimumab: ORR 0% in pMMR CRC; single case report of 313-day response (Zhang et al. Cancer Gene Ther 2024)
- **Evidence level: MECHANISTIC PLAUSIBILITY; clinical translation has failed in unselected patients**

### 5. WHAT A WELL-DESIGNED MSS CRC IO TRIAL WOULD LOOK LIKE

Based on the accumulated lessons from all four tiers, a well-designed MSS CRC IO trial would incorporate the following elements:

**Element 1: Mandatory biomarker-based patient selection (not broad enrollment)**
- **Exclude liver-metastatic patients** or stratify heavily by liver metastasis status (the single most important clinical predictor)
- **Enrich for immune-inflamed phenotype:** Immunoscore-IC ≥2, or DetermaIO-high, or CD8+ T-cell density above threshold
- **Consider pTMB enrichment:** pTMB ≥28 variants/Mb identifies a rare but responsive subgroup
- **Consider disease burden:** Low ctDNA at treatment initiation may predict better outcomes

**Element 2: Address the dominant suppressive mechanisms, not just PD-1**
- **TGF-β pathway inhibition** is mechanistically essential for immune-excluded MSS CRC (galunisertib, bintrafusp alfa, or SMAD pathway inhibitors)
- **VEGF/VEGFR inhibition** to reduce myeloid immunosuppression (bevacizumab, cabozantinib, zanzalintinib)
- **MDSC depletion or reprogramming** (low-dose cyclophosphamide, CSF1R inhibitors, or CXCR2 inhibitors for KRAS-mutant CRC)

**Element 3: Antigen-directed component to prime T cells**
- Personalized neoantigen vaccine or shared antigen vaccine to generate tumor-specific T cells
- Timing: concurrent with or immediately after chemotherapy-induced immunogenic cell death
- Platform: mRNA or viral vector for robust T-cell induction

**Element 4: Appropriate setting and timing**
- **Minimal residual disease (ctDNA+) setting** may be more permissive than established metastatic disease
- **Maintenance after induction chemotherapy** (not concurrent with full-dose chemo) to allow immune reconstitution
- **Non-liver-metastatic or liver-metastasis-controlled** (post-resection or ablation) setting

**Element 5: Pharmacodynamic endpoints as co-primary or key secondary**
- ctDNA clearance as early pharmacodynamic endpoint
- Tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte density (pre/post biopsy)
- Neoantigen-specific T-cell responses in peripheral blood
- These endpoints allow early go/no-go decisions before waiting for OS

**Element 6: Adaptive design with biomarker-driven enrichment**
- Start with broad enrollment but pre-specify biomarker-enriched analysis
- Allow adaptive enrichment to the responding subgroup
- Use Bayesian or adaptive randomization to identify the optimal combination

**Prototype trial design:**
> Phase II randomized trial of [neoantigen vaccine + TGF-β inhibitor + PD-1 blockade] vs. [standard of care] in MSS mCRC patients WITHOUT liver metastases (or with liver metastases controlled by resection/ablation), enriched for Immunoscore-IC ≥2 or DetermaIO-high. Primary endpoint: PFS. Key secondary: ctDNA clearance rate, neoantigen-specific T-cell response, OS. Stratification by KRAS mutation status, prior lines of therapy, and ctDNA burden.

## WHAT COULD NOT BE FOUND

1. **IMpower150 CRC subgroup data:** IMpower150 was exclusively an NSCLC trial. No CRC-specific subgroup data exists from this trial. The search confirmed this is not a relevant comparator for CRC.

2. **TG4010 in CRC:** TG4010 (MVA-MUC1-IL2 vaccine) has been studied in NSCLC but no CRC-specific clinical trial data was identified in the literature search.

3. **Prospective biomarker-enriched MSS CRC IO trial results:** No completed prospective trial has enrolled MSS CRC patients pre-selected by Immunoscore-IC, pTMB ≥28, or non-liver-metastatic status as the primary enrollment criterion. All biomarker signals are from post-hoc subgroup analyses.

4. **SMAD4-stratified IO trial:** No clinical trial has prospectively stratified MSS CRC patients by SMAD4 status for IO therapy. SMAD4 as a predictive biomarker for IO resistance remains at the level of mechanistic plausibility.

5. **Randomized phase III data for RIN (regorafenib + ipilimumab + nivolumab):** The RIN data is from a phase I non-randomized trial. No phase III randomized data exists for this combination.

6. **STELLAR-303 results:** This phase III trial (zanzalintinib + atezolizumab vs. regorafenib in MSS/MSI-low mCRC) is ongoing; results not yet available.
