# An honestly-tiered prognostic and diagnostic biomarker subsystem for high-grade serous ovarian carcinoma: reproduction, external replication, and a self-audit of what the public data actually support

**Fahad Kiani¹** [ORCID TBD], [co-authors TBD]

¹ Brenus Pharma / CrisPRO platform. Correspondence: Fahad Kiani ([email TBD]).

**Significance.** Translational biomarker software accumulates models faster than it retires them, and a model's *label* ("validated", "prognostic") often outruns the evidence behind it. We take the opposite discipline. We re-execute — not merely re-cite — every analysis in a four-model ovarian-cancer biomarker subsystem (a published survival signature, a molecular-subtype classifier, and two pathway-panel scans), extend each external test to every adequately-powered public cohort available to us, and assign each model the *tier its own re-run earns*. The audit moves tiers in **both** directions: one pathway panel (DDR) rises to a "Consider" tier on independent replication; a reproduced literature signature (Riester) is corrected **downward** to discovery_only once we establish that its only strict cohort sits inside the signature's own development compendium; one pathway panel (PI3K/mTOR) is a confirmed null after a fair 17-cohort test; and the diagnostic classifier (consensusOV), now fully re-executed from source, remains a composition-confounded descriptive tool. Nothing is described as clinically validated. The contribution is not a new biomarker but a **reproducible, receipt-backed tiering discipline** that makes a discovery-stage subsystem safe to build on — including the discipline to demote our own prior claims when independence does not hold.

## Abstract

**Background.** Discovery-stage biomarker platforms routinely bundle heterogeneous models — reproduced literature signatures, off-the-shelf classifiers, and in-house exploratory panels — under a single "biomarker subsystem". The risk is not that any one model is wrong, but that weak, null, confounded, and non-independent results are presented at the same confidence as genuinely replicated ones. We audited a four-model ovarian-cancer subsystem to determine, by re-execution, what each model actually supports.

**Approach.** All analyses were run in R against the curated MetaGxOvarian collection (26 ovarian-cancer expression cohorts). We (i) reproduced a published 19-gene stromal survival signature (Riester *et al.* [3]) as a frozen-coefficient prognostic score and evaluated it by 5-fold out-of-fold (OOF) cross-validation with in-fold feature selection on TCGA, then applied the frozen coefficients — without refitting — to every independent cohort with adequate power (n ≥ 40, events ≥ 15); (ii) **re-executed** a consensus molecular-subtype classifier (consensusOV [4]) — installed from the authors' source repository after it proved absent from the current Bioconductor release — and tested it for confounding by tumour purity; and (iii) extended two unsupervised pathway-panel scores (a DNA-damage-response [DDR] panel and a PI3K/mTOR panel) from an original four-cohort scan to all 17 adequately-powered cohorts. Concordance was measured with the C-index (95% CI, Noether method) and a Cox hazard ratio (HR) per 1 SD of the linear predictor, with proportional-hazards (PH) testing throughout. A single pre-specified tier rule was applied uniformly: **Strong** = strict signal (C-index CI excludes 0.5 *and* HR CI excludes 1) in ≥ 2 *independent* cohorts; **Consider** = strict signal in ≥ 1 *independent* cohort; otherwise **discovery_only** — with independence assessed at the level of feature selection, not only coefficient fitting.

**Results.** The Riester score reproduced its OOF benchmark on TCGA (C-index 0.539, 95% CI 0.500–0.579, p = 0.050; three non-zero frozen coefficients — NUAK1, TGFBR2, LOX). Across 15 independent adequate-power cohorts it produced a strict signal in exactly one (GSE9891: C-index 0.625, 95% CI 0.569–0.681, p = 1.2 × 10⁻⁵; HR 1.36, 95% CI 1.13–1.63), with 12 of 15 cohorts leaning weakly positive but with CIs spanning 0.5. Critically, GSE9891 (Tothill 2008) is one of the datasets in Riester's own 1525-sample signature-development compendium [3, 7]; because the 19 panel genes were *selected* on a compendium that included this cohort, GSE9891 is not an independent test of the signature even though our coefficients were re-fit cleanly on TCGA. With no strict signal in any genuinely independent cohort, the Riester score is assigned **discovery_only** — a correction *downward* from a prior internal "Consider". The DDR panel, extended from 4 to 17 cohorts, produced a strict signal in one cohort absent from the original scan (GSE17260: C-index 0.611, 95% CI 0.521–0.702; HR 1.61, 95% CI 1.15–2.25, p = 0.005) → **Consider**, tempered by one significantly inverse cohort (PMID17290060: C-index 0.400, upper CI 0.469). Because DDR is a *de-novo canonical gene panel* (not a literature signature whose features were selected on these cohorts), the feature-selection-leakage concern that demotes Riester does not apply. The PI3K/mTOR panel produced a strict signal in 0 of 17 cohorts, with one significantly inverse cohort (PMID17290060: C-index 0.383, upper CI 0.455, p = 0.0014) and no stable direction → **discovery_only (confirmed null)**. The consensusOV classifier, re-executed on all 536 TCGA samples, produced a stable subtype mixture (DIF 0.297, IMR 0.274, MES 0.237, PRO 0.192; cross-cohort total-variation distance to TCGA ≤ 0.077) but was composition-confounded: the mesenchymal (MES) subtype tracked low tumour purity / high stroma (Kruskal–Wallis p = 4.9 × 10⁻⁶ for tumour purity, p = 2.8 × 10⁻⁷ for stroma; MES mean tumour purity 76.1% vs grand mean 80.9%) → **discovery_only (descriptive)**.

**Interpretation.** A re-execution-based audit changed the honest conclusions of this subsystem in both directions: it *upgraded* one pathway panel (DDR) to Consider on independent replication the original narrower scan had not surfaced, and it *downgraded* a reproduced literature signature (Riester) once we established that its only strict cohort was not independent of its feature-selection step. It held a second pathway panel (PI3K/mTOR) at null rather than promoting a near-0.5 scan, and it converted the diagnostic classifier from a "not-re-executable" caveat into a fully re-run but composition-confounded descriptive tool. We present the subsystem as discovery-stage, with one Consider-tier prognostic signal (DDR), one reported null (PI3K/mTOR), one non-independent literature signature held at discovery_only (Riester), and one confound-limited descriptive diagnostic (consensusOV). None of these is clinically validated. The companion within-patient copy-number study (dispersed-CIN architecture and a PTEN nomination) is cross-referenced, not merged; the relationship between the PI3K/mTOR expression null and the companion's structural PTEN loss is discussed explicitly as a *modality distinction*, not as a hidden signal.

**Keywords.** high-grade serous ovarian carcinoma; prognostic signature; molecular subtype; DNA damage response; PI3K/mTOR; external replication; reproducibility; feature-selection leakage; C-index; tumour purity confounding; discovery-stage biomarker.

## 1. Introduction

Biomarker discovery in ovarian cancer has produced a large literature of expression signatures and molecular classifications, yet very few reach clinical use [1, 2]. A recurring reason is a mismatch between the *evidence* a model carries and the *label* it is given. A signature that is genuinely prognostic in its discovery cohort may be near-null elsewhere; a molecular-subtype call may be reproducible yet driven by tumour purity rather than tumour biology; an in-house pathway score assembled for exploration may drift into being described as a validated readout; and a cohort reused from a signature's own training compendium can masquerade as independent validation. When several such models are bundled into a "biomarker subsystem" and surfaced through the same software interface, these distinctions are easily lost.

This paper is deliberately not a new-biomarker paper. It is a **self-audit** of an existing four-model ovarian-cancer subsystem, conducted by re-executing the underlying analysis code rather than re-reading its prior outputs, and it reports each model at the tier its own re-run earns. The four models are: (1) a reproduction of the Riester *et al.* 19-gene stromal survival signature [3] as a frozen-coefficient prognostic score; (2) the consensusOV consensus molecular-subtype classifier [4]; and (3, 4) two unsupervised pathway-panel scores — a DNA-damage-response (DDR) panel and a PI3K/mTOR panel — used as baseline discovery scans.

Three commitments structure the work. First, **evidence decides the tier.** We pre-specify a single tier rule and apply it uniformly; a model rises only if independent-cohort replication clears the bar, and a model that is genuinely null after a fair, adequately-powered test stays null and is reported as null. Presenting a real null as a "Consider" or "Strong" result would be the exact failure this audit exists to prevent. Second, **independence is assessed honestly.** A cohort counts as independent replication only if it did not contribute to the model's feature selection; a signature whose genes were chosen on a compendium cannot be validated on a member of that compendium, regardless of how cleanly its coefficients are subsequently re-fit. Third, **every tier assignment is receipt-backed.** Each re-run writes a machine-readable receipt next to the original result; where a number does not reproduce, or where a prior claim does not survive an independence check, that is treated as a finding and corrected in the open — including corrections *against* our own earlier conclusions.

The motivating question is therefore narrow and practical: *when each model in this subsystem is re-run honestly across all the public data we can bring to bear, which ones actually carry an independent signal, which are confounded, which are non-independent, and which are null?* The answer determines what a downstream platform is entitled to claim.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Cohorts and expression data
All expression analyses used the curated MetaGxOvarian resource, which assembles ovarian-cancer transcriptomic cohorts with harmonised clinical annotation [5]. The full collection (26 cohorts) was loaded once and cached locally as per-cohort expression sets to make re-runs deterministic and inexpensive. For each analysis, cohorts were included as **independent adequate-power replication cohorts** only if they had n ≥ 40 samples and ≥ 15 survival events; cohorts below either threshold were recorded as `insufficient_power` and excluded from tiering. TCGA-OVARIAN served as the training cohort for the prognostic score (n = 521, 281 events) and was never counted as an independent replication cohort.

### 2.2 Expression pre-processing
Within each cohort, probes were collapsed to HUGO gene symbols by averaging across probes mapping to the same symbol, and each gene was z-scored within cohort. Genes in a model's panel that were absent from a given cohort were dropped for that cohort, and the number of genes present versus expected was recorded per cohort so that partial-panel cohorts are transparent.

### 2.3 Reproduced prognostic score (Riester signature)
The 19-gene stromal signature panel [3] (POSTN, CXCL14, FAP, NUAK1, PTCH1, THBS2, TGFBR2, TIMP3, COL11A1, COL5A1, COL6A3, LOX, AEBP1, INHBA, CTSK, DCN, LUM, FBN1, ANGPTL2) was fit on TCGA as a penalised Cox model (elastic net, α = 0.5, λ = λ.min) with **feature selection performed inside each cross-validation fold** to avoid selection leakage within the TCGA benchmark. Model performance on TCGA was estimated by 5-fold **out-of-fold** cross-validation; the reported C-index is the OOF value, never an in-sample fit. The penalised fit retained three non-zero coefficients (NUAK1 = 0.093836, TGFBR2 = 0.075434, LOX = 0.00072); 18 of the 19 panel genes were present in TCGA (PTCH1 absent). These three coefficients were then **frozen** and applied, without any refitting, to every independent cohort as a fixed linear predictor — the correct design for external replication of the *coefficients*. Independence of each replication cohort from the signature's *feature-selection* step is treated separately in §2.6.

### 2.4 Pathway-panel discovery scans (DDR, PI3K/mTOR)
Each panel score was computed as the unsupervised **mean z-score** of its member genes within each cohort — an exploratory score that is *not* fitted to outcome. The canonical panels used were: DDR = BRCA1, BRCA2, ATM, ATR, CHEK1, CHEK2, RAD51, PALB2, FANCA, PARP1, MRE11, NBN, RAD50; PI3K/mTOR = PIK3CA, PIK3CB, PIK3R1, PTEN, AKT1, AKT2, MTOR, RPTOR, RICTOR, TSC1, TSC2, STK11, INPP4B. Because these are de-novo canonical gene lists rather than signatures whose members were selected on the replication cohorts, every adequate-power MetaGxOvarian cohort is an independent test of the *panel score* (see §2.6).

### 2.5 Diagnostic classifier re-execution (consensusOV)
The consensusOV classifier [4] was **re-executed from source**, not re-cited. At the installed Bioconductor release (3.20, R 4.4.3) neither `consensusOV` nor its dependency `genefu` is present in the release repository; `BiocManager::available("consensusOV")` returns empty and no source tarball resolves from the standard archive URLs. Rather than record this as a reproducibility caveat, both packages were installed directly from the authors' source repository (`bhklab/genefu` then `bhklab/consensusOV` via `remotes::install_github`, pulling dependencies iC10TrainingData, pamr, mclust, amap, AIMS, iC10). The random-forest consensus classifier was then trained and used to classify **all 536 TCGA-OVARIAN samples** with Entrez identifiers mapped from the cohort feature annotation. Subtype calls were summarised as fractions and cross-tabulated against the cohort's percent-tumour / percent-stromal / percent-normal annotation to test the composition confound. The same classifier was applied to two additional cohorts (GSE9891, GSE32062) to quantify cross-cohort stability of the subtype mixture by total-variation distance (TVD) against TCGA.

### 2.6 Independence and the tier rule
Concordance was measured with the C-index (95% CI, Noether method, `survcomp::concordance.index`) and a Cox HR per 1 SD of the scaled linear predictor (`coxph`), with the PH assumption tested by `cox.zph`. A **strict signal** requires the C-index 95% CI to exclude 0.5 *and* the HR 95% CI to exclude 1. The tier rule was applied uniformly: **Strong** = strict signal in ≥ 2 *independent* cohorts; **Consider** = strict signal in ≥ 1 *independent* cohort; otherwise **discovery_only**.

The word *independent* is load-bearing and is assessed at the level of **feature selection**, not only coefficient fitting:

- For the **Riester signature**, the 19 genes were selected by the original authors on a 1525-sample meta-analytic compendium of 13 datasets using a leave-one-dataset-out procedure [3]. Any MetaGxOvarian cohort that was a member of that compendium is therefore *not* an independent test of the signature, even if our elastic-net coefficients are re-fit only on TCGA. Compendium membership was determined against the published description of the Riester development set [3] and corroborated by the companion comparative meta-analysis of late-stage ovarian signatures [7], which reuses the same expression compendium and documents its constituent datasets, including the Tothill (GSE9891) cohort; the GEO series year of each cached cohort was used as a transparent, verifiable proxy where a dataset-level membership table could not be retrieved directly (all 15 cached adequate-power cohorts predate the 2014 signature and belong to the curatedOvarianData/MetaGxOvarian era from which the compendium was drawn).
- For the **DDR and PI3K/mTOR panels**, the gene lists are de-novo canonical pathway memberships not derived from these cohorts, so there is no feature-selection dependency and every adequate-power cohort is an independent test of the panel score.

### 2.7 Audit-harness corrections
Two defects in the original audit harness were corrected before tiering: a probe-collapse edge case for cohorts with duplicated symbols, and a survival-object construction error in one cohort loader. Corrected outputs reproduce the original per-cohort numbers where those numbers were correct, and the corrected values are the ones reported here and written to the receipts.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Reproduced prognostic score reproduces its benchmark on TCGA
Re-executing the Riester frozen-coefficient score on TCGA reproduced its OOF benchmark: C-index 0.539 (95% CI 0.500–0.579, p = 0.050) with three non-zero coefficients (NUAK1, TGFBR2, LOX) and 18 of 19 panel genes present (PTCH1 absent), n = 521, 281 events. This is a modest but real within-TCGA prognostic signal and is consistent with the original report's magnitude for a stromal survival score [3]. It is reported as the OOF value; no in-sample fit is used as a headline metric.

### 3.2 The Riester signature has no *independent* strict signal → discovery_only (corrected downward)
Applying the frozen coefficients without refitting to 15 independent adequate-power cohorts, the score leaned weakly positive in most cohorts (12 of 15 with C-index > 0.5) but cleared the strict bar (C-index CI excludes 0.5 and HR CI excludes 1) in exactly one: **GSE9891** (C-index 0.625, 95% CI 0.569–0.681, p = 1.2 × 10⁻⁵; HR 1.36, 95% CI 1.13–1.63), with a borderline PH result in that cohort.

The decisive audit finding is that **GSE9891 is not independent of the signature.** GSE9891 is the Tothill 2008 cohort, one of the datasets in Riester's 1525-sample signature-development compendium [3, 7]. The 19 panel genes were *selected* on a compendium that included this cohort; evaluating the signature on GSE9891 therefore re-uses data that informed the choice of features, and the strong C-index there partly reflects that leakage rather than out-of-distribution generalisation. This is a subtler dependency than coefficient over-fitting — our coefficients were re-fit cleanly on TCGA — but it is a real one, and it operates at the feature-selection level.

No genuinely independent (post-development, non-compendium) adequate-power ovarian cohort was available in the cache to rescue the tier: every one of the 15 cached adequate-power cohorts belongs to the curatedOvarianData/MetaGxOvarian era from which the compendium was drawn and predates the 2014 signature (Tothill 2008, Bonome 2008, Crijns 2009, Mok 2010, Ferriss 2011, Denkert 2009, Bentink 2012, Yoshihara 2010–2012, Karlan 2013, Dressman 2007, and others). With no strict signal in any independent cohort, the honest tier is **discovery_only**, with a documented consistent weak-positive lean (12/15 cohorts C-index 0.51–0.56, all with CIs crossing 0.5). This is an explicit **downward correction** of a prior internal "Consider" call, and it is the clearest illustration in this audit of independence — not raw effect size — deciding a tier. It is also a genuine data-availability ceiling: pulling additional curatedOvarianData/MetaGxOvarian cohorts would hit the same compendium contamination, not resolve it.

### 3.3 DDR panel earns Consider on an independent cohort
Extending the DDR mean-z panel score from the original four-cohort scan to all 17 adequate-power cohorts produced a strict signal in one cohort that was **not** in the original scan: **GSE17260** (C-index 0.611, 95% CI 0.521–0.702; HR 1.61, 95% CI 1.15–2.25, p = 0.005). Because the DDR panel is a de-novo canonical gene list rather than a signature whose members were selected on these cohorts, GSE17260 is a genuinely independent test and the feature-selection-leakage concern that demotes Riester does not apply. The tier is therefore **Consider**, tempered by one significantly inverse cohort (PMID17290060: C-index 0.400, 95% CI 0.330–0.469; HR 0.73, p = 0.017), which keeps the panel below the Strong bar (a single independent strict cohort, with a documented counter-signal).

### 3.4 PI3K/mTOR panel is a confirmed null → discovery_only
The PI3K/mTOR mean-z panel score, tested across the same 17 adequate-power cohorts, produced a strict signal in **0 of 17**. Its only significant cohort pointed the *wrong* way (PMID17290060: C-index 0.383, 95% CI 0.311–0.455, p = 0.0014 — significantly inverse), and no cohort combined a C-index CI excluding 0.5 with an HR CI excluding 1 in the positive direction. There is no stable prognostic direction. The honest tier is **discovery_only (confirmed null)**. This is reported as a null, not smoothed toward "near-positive"; a panel that is null after a fair 17-cohort test is exactly the result the audit is designed to report faithfully. §4 discusses why this expression-panel null is *not* in conflict with the companion study's structural PTEN observation.

### 3.5 consensusOV classifier — re-executed, reproducible mixture, composition-confounded → discovery_only (descriptive)
The consensusOV classifier was re-executed from source (§2.5) and classified all 536 TCGA samples. The subtype mixture was stable and summed to unity: DIF 0.297 (n = 159), IMR 0.274 (n = 147), MES 0.237 (n = 127), PRO 0.192 (n = 103). Applied to two further cohorts, the mixture was similar (cross-cohort TVD to TCGA: GSE9891 0.031, GSE32062 0.077), i.e. the classifier produces a reproducible per-cohort *mixture*.

However, on these fresh per-patient calls the mesenchymal subtype tracked **low tumour purity and high stromal content**: across TCGA, percent tumour cells differed by subtype at Kruskal–Wallis p = 4.9 × 10⁻⁶ and percent stromal cells at p = 2.8 × 10⁻⁷ (percent normal cells n.s., p = 0.23), with the MES group mean tumour purity (76.1%) the clear low outlier against a cohort grand mean of 80.9% (MES stroma mean 18.3% vs grand mean 12.4%; n ≈ 516 with purity annotation, independently re-read from the cached phenotype data and matching the receipt). The subtype call is therefore a reproducible mixture that is **confounded by sample composition** rather than a validated per-patient biological classification. Tier: **discovery_only (descriptive)**.

Relative to the prior audit pass, the substantive change here is that the classifier is now **fully re-executed** — the earlier "could not be re-run in this Bioconductor environment / partial-verified" caveat is withdrawn. Re-execution did not change the tier (the composition confound persists on fresh calls), but it removes a reproducibility gap: any downstream user can now regenerate the per-patient subtype labels from source. The remaining limitation is biological (purity confounding), not computational.

The net effect of the audit on the four-model subsystem (plus the cross-referenced companion signal) is summarised in **Table 1**. One pathway panel moved up to Consider on independent replication; one reproduced literature signature moved down to discovery_only on an independence finding; one pathway panel was held at a reported null; the diagnostic classifier was re-executed and held as a confound-limited descriptive tool.

## 4. Discussion

Re-executing this subsystem, rather than re-reading it, changed its honest conclusions in **both** directions — which is the point of a self-audit that is willing to correct its own prior claims. The audit *upgraded* one pathway panel that a narrower analysis had left understated: the DDR mean-z score carries a strict, independent signal in GSE17260 that the original four-cohort scan could not see, and because DDR is a de-novo canonical panel that signal survives the independence test. The audit simultaneously *downgraded* a reproduced literature signature: the Riester score's only strict cohort, GSE9891, is a member of the signature's own development compendium [3, 7], so it cannot serve as independent validation, and with no independent strict cohort available the score is discovery_only rather than Consider. Demoting a signal we had previously elevated is uncomfortable, but a tiering discipline that only ever ratchets upward is not a discipline at all.

The PI3K/mTOR result is a genuine null and is reported as one: 0 of 17 cohorts strict, with the only significant cohort inverted. This is worth stating plainly because it would have been easy — and wrong — to describe a panel whose cohort C-indices scatter around 0.5 as "near-positive" or "trending". It is not; it is null, and its single significant cohort actively contradicts the hypothesised direction.

It is equally important to separate this expression-panel null from the distinct, lesion-level observation in the companion within-patient copy-number study. In that study, Subject 260 acquires a **structural PTEN copy-number loss** at platinum relapse (relative copy number 0.967 → 0.692; neutral → loss under the analysis's band rule), nominating the PI3K/AKT/mTOR axis in that single patient. These two observations are **not in conflict, and their relationship is a modality distinction, not a hidden signal.** The PI3K/mTOR panel score is a bulk, cross-sectional, 13-gene mean-z expression readout averaged over a whole tumour and over a whole cohort. A single-gene, single-patient, relapse-acquired *structural DNA* event is (i) diluted roughly one-in-thirteen inside a panel mean, (ii) at the DNA copy-number level rather than the bulk-mRNA level the panel measures, and (iii) private to one subject rather than a cohort-wide axis. A bulk expression panel is simply the wrong instrument to detect a private structural lesion of this kind, and the fact that it does not is expected. We state this explicitly as a scientific distinction between measurement modalities; we do **not** claim that the null "really" contains the PTEN signal, and we do not treat the companion lesion as evidence that the expression panel works. The correct reading is narrower and more useful: the negative expression-panel result and the positive single-patient structural result address different objects, and a platform should not use the bulk PI3K/mTOR expression score to reason about structural PTEN loss.

The diagnostic result is a cautionary example of a third kind. The consensusOV subtype mixture is highly reproducible — and now fully re-executable from source — but reproducibility is not validity. The mesenchymal subtype's tight association with low tumour purity means that a per-patient "MES" call partly reports how much stroma was in the block, not only the tumour's intrinsic program. A subsystem that surfaced this label as a validated diagnostic would be over-claiming; we keep it descriptive. The improvement over the prior pass is computational transparency, not a change of tier: the earlier "classifier not re-executable" caveat is withdrawn because the classifier now runs end-to-end, but the biological confound that caps the tier is unchanged.

These results position the subsystem accurately for downstream use. The one Consider-tier prognostic signal (DDR) is appropriate as a **hypothesis-strengthening feature** — an input that can contribute to a composite or nominate a stratification hypothesis — but not as a standalone validated test. The Riester score should be treated as discovery_only pending a genuinely independent (post-2014, non-compendium) ovarian cohort. The PI3K/mTOR expression panel should not be used as a prognostic readout at all. And the consensusOV subtype label should be used descriptively, with tumour purity carried as a covariate.

**Relation to companion work.** The within-patient dispersed-CIN architecture study (five matched diagnosis→relapse HGSOC pairs; dispersed stochastic CIN rather than focal catastrophe; the Subject 260 PTEN nomination) is a **companion manuscript** and is deliberately **not merged** here. Merging real-data copy-number findings with public-cohort expression tiering would blur two very different evidence bases and reintroduce the contamination risk that separate reporting avoids. The two are cross-referenced so a reader can see the whole program while each stands on its own evidence. The resistance-prophet specification that consumes these tiers downstream is likewise cross-referenced, not embedded.

## 5. Limitations

1. **Discovery-stage, not clinically validated.** Nothing in this subsystem is a validated clinical test. "Consider" denotes a reproducible independent-cohort signal, not clinical utility; "discovery_only" denotes exploratory or non-independent status.
2. **The one Consider signal is single-cohort-strict.** The DDR panel clears the strict bar in exactly one independent cohort (GSE17260) and carries a significantly inverse counter-cohort; it does not meet the ≥ 2-cohort bar for Strong.
3. **Riester independence ceiling.** The Riester downgrade rests on the finding that no independent (non-compendium, post-2014) adequate-power ovarian cohort exists in the available cache; the weak positive lean (12/15) is consistent with a real but small stromal effect that we cannot confirm out-of-distribution. This is a data-availability ceiling, not a claim that the signature is false.
4. **Compendium membership determined by proxy.** A dataset-level membership table for the Riester development set could not be retrieved directly; membership was established from the published description in [3] and the companion comparative meta-analysis [7], with GEO series year used as a transparent proxy. The proxy is defensible (all cached cohorts predate the 2014 signature and are curatedOvarianData-era) but is not a substitute for the original table.
5. **PI3K/mTOR null is a panel-score null.** The confirmed null applies to the unsupervised mean-z expression panel as a prognostic score; it does not speak to specific PI3K-axis structural lesions (see §4 modality distinction) or to therapeutic targeting of the pathway.
6. **Purity confounding is quantified but not corrected.** We demonstrate the MES↔purity association on freshly re-executed calls; we do not attempt a purity-adjusted re-classification, which would be the natural next step now that the classifier is re-executable.
7. **Unsupervised panel scores.** The DDR and PI3K/mTOR scores are mean-z panel scores, not outcome-fitted models; their tiers reflect independent-cohort replication of an unsupervised score only.
8. **Public expression data.** All expression analyses use curated public microarray/RNA cohorts; batch and platform heterogeneity across MetaGxOvarian cohorts is mitigated by within-cohort z-scoring and frozen-coefficient application but not eliminated.

## 6. Data and reproducibility

All expression analyses used the public MetaGxOvarian collection. Each model's re-run produced a machine-readable receipt recording per-cohort n, events, C-index and 95% CI, HR and 95% CI, PH-test p-value, genes present versus expected, and the tier decision, alongside the original receipts for diff. The consensusOV classifier was re-executed after installing `genefu` and `consensusOV` from their authors' source repository (both absent from the installed Bioconductor release); the re-execution receipt records the install route, the 536-sample subtype counts, and the composition-confound statistics. The two audit-harness defects described in §2.7 were corrected before tiering, and corrected outputs reproduce the original per-cohort numbers. Figures were regenerated directly from the re-audit receipts. Companion within-patient copy-number findings are reported separately and are cross-referenced, not merged.
