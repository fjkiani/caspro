## 2. OPPORTUNITY ASSESSMENT

### Market Context: Why MSS CRC Is a High-Value Target for CrisPRO

Microsatellite-stable (MSS) colorectal cancer constitutes approximately 95% of all metastatic CRC — roughly 140,000 new metastatic cases per year in the US and EU combined [Agent 3]. It is the single largest unmet need in gastrointestinal oncology and one of the most commercially significant unsolved problems in immuno-oncology. Every major checkpoint inhibitor program has failed in unselected MSS CRC: nivolumab monotherapy achieved 0% ORR in the MSS cohort of CheckMate 142 (n=74); MEK inhibition + atezolizumab failed in IMblaze370; dual checkpoint blockade + bevacizumab showed no benefit in MODUL; and the GRANITE neoantigen vaccine trial failed its primary endpoint in unselected MSS CRC at ASCO 2025 [Agent 3, PROVEN EFFICACY]. The accumulated failure landscape has created a clear scientific consensus: the path forward requires precision patient selection, not broader treatment. This is precisely the problem CrisPRO is built to solve. A successful engagement in MSS CRC IO — even a partial one that identifies a responding subgroup — would be a landmark commercial and scientific reference case for CrisPRO.

### Program Stage: Why NOW Is the Right Engagement Window

Brenus Pharma is at the most strategically valuable moment for a computational partner to engage. Phase Ia of BreAK CRC-001 (NCT06934538) is active and recruiting at five French sites; the first six patients have been evaluated with a median follow-up of six months; and the company has guided that complete Phase Ia safety and exploratory efficacy data across all dose cohorts is expected Q2 2026 [Agent 1, VERIFIED]. Phase IIa design decisions — including the primary endpoint, patient selection criteria, and biomarker stratification strategy — must be made in the next three to six months. This is the window in which CrisPRO's input has maximum leverage: a Phase IIa protocol amendment is far easier to execute than a Phase III redesign, and the exploratory biomarker data from Phase I patients is being collected right now. If CrisPRO engages after Phase IIa opens, the opportunity to shape the trial design will have passed. The company has €30M raised (Series A closed September 2024 at €22.2M), four to five active recruiting sites, and a scientific advisory board that includes Alexander Eggermont (former Director General of Gustave Roussy) and François Ghiringhelli (CGFL Dijon, BreAK-CRC study coordinator) — both of whom are sophisticated enough to understand the value of computational biomarker analysis [Agent 1, VERIFIED].

### Competitive Landscape: Who Else Might Be Competing for This Engagement

Brenus is a small, privately held French biotech with no disclosed computational oncology partnerships. The company's Series A investor base is predominantly regional (Angelor, UI Investissement, Crédit Agricole, Noshaq, Investsud) with no major US or global pharma strategic investor [Agent 1, VERIFIED]. This means Brenus is unlikely to have an existing relationship with a large computational oncology platform (e.g., Tempus, Foundation Medicine, Guardant). However, the SAB includes Mark Yarchoan (Johns Hopkins) and Julien Taieb (Georges Pompidou), both of whom have existing academic collaborations that could evolve into competing analytical partnerships. The most credible competitive threat is an academic collaboration with CGFL Dijon (Ghiringhelli's institution) or Institut Bergonié (Italiano's institution), both of which have translational research infrastructure. CrisPRO's advantage over academic collaborators is speed, commercial focus, and the ability to deliver a pre-specified biomarker analysis plan that is regulatory-grade — not just scientifically interesting. The engagement window is approximately six months before Phase IIa design is locked.

## 3. SCIENTIFIC FIT SUMMARY

The following three HIGH-fit capabilities from the Agent 5 fit-gap analysis represent the core commercial argument for CrisPRO's engagement. These are not generic platform claims — they are specific, time-sensitive needs that the BreAK CRC-001 program has right now.

### Fit 1: Phase IIa Adaptive Enrichment Design (Priority Rank 1 in Agent 5)

**The problem:** The current Phase IIa design is a single-arm trial comparing the 12-month PFS rate against historical FOLFOX benchmarks in unselected MSS CRC patients. This design has a near-certain probability of failure. The GRANITE neoantigen vaccine trial — the closest comparator — failed its primary endpoint in unselected MSS CRC at ASCO 2025 [Agent 3, PROVEN EFFICACY]. The historical FOLFOX benchmark for 12-month PFS in first-line MSS mCRC is approximately 50–55%, and with no concurrent control arm, any observed PFS rate cannot be causally attributed to STC-1010 [Agent 2, ANALYTICAL INTERPRETATION]. The single-arm design is the most dangerous structural weakness in the program.

**CrisPRO's value proposition:** CrisPRO can deliver an adaptive enrichment design for Phase IIa that (a) pre-specifies the biomarker-defined subgroup most likely to benefit (non-liver-metastatic, SMAD4-intact, low TGF-β signature patients — the composite hypothesis from Agent 4); (b) incorporates ctDNA clearance as an early pharmacodynamic co-primary endpoint, following the GRANITE low-ctDNA subgroup signal (HR 0.50, exploratory) [Agent 3, PHARMACODYNAMIC ACTIVITY]; (c) provides power calculations for the biomarker-enriched primary analysis; and (d) includes a Bayesian adaptive design framework that allows early go/no-go decisions based on biomarker-defined subgroups. This is not a generic statistical service — it is a purpose-built solution to the specific design failure mode that has killed every prior MSS CRC IO trial.

### Fit 2: Exploratory Biomarker Analysis Plan for Phase I Data (Priority Rank 3 in Agent 5)

**The problem:** BreAK CRC-001 is collecting an extensive exploratory biomarker dataset — cytokine profiles, immunophenotyping, PBMC markers, ctDNA dynamics, HLA expression, tumor necrosis, immune infiltration, and TLS evolution [Agent 2, VERIFIED]. However, the assay platforms are not specified in the public protocol, there are no pre-specified response thresholds, and there is no pre-specified analysis plan [Agent 2, NOT FOUND IN PUBLIC SOURCES]. Without a pre-specified analysis plan, the Phase I biomarker data will be analyzed post-hoc and will not be credible to regulators or sophisticated partners. The Phase Ia complete dataset is expected Q2 2026 — the analysis plan must be in place before that data is locked.

**CrisPRO's value proposition:** CrisPRO's biomarker stratification engine can integrate the multi-omic data streams from Phase I patients with the published MSS CRC biomarker literature (Agent 4's six-domain biology map) to generate a ranked, pre-specified biomarker hypothesis for Phase IIa patient selection. The five-biomarker composite hypothesis from Agent 4 — absence of active liver metastases, low TGF-β exclusion signature, SMAD4 intact, intact MHC-I/TAP expression, KRAS-WT or BRAF V600E MSS — provides the mechanistic framework [Agent 4, MECHANISTIC HYPOTHESIS]. CrisPRO can translate this into a standardized assay panel, pre-specified response thresholds, and a regulatory-grade analysis plan that Brenus can embed in the Phase IIa protocol amendment.

### Fit 3: Liver Metastasis Stratification & Non-Liver-Metastatic Subgroup Analysis (Priority Rank 2 in Agent 5)

**The problem:** The BreAK CRC-001 protocol does not specify liver metastasis burden limits, does not stratify by liver metastasis status, and does not track liver metastasis as a covariate in efficacy analyses [Agent 2, NOT FOUND IN PUBLIC SOURCES]. This is the single most consequential design gap in the program. Active liver metastases are present in approximately 50–70% of mCRC patients [Agent 4, VALIDATED CLINICAL OBSERVATION] and are the strongest single predictor of IO failure in MSS CRC — with near-zero response rates in liver-metastatic patients across REGONIVO (ORR ~0% with liver mets vs. 33% without), RIN (ORR ~5% with liver mets vs. ~40% without), and CCTG CO.26 (OS HR ~0.90 with liver mets vs. ~0.50 without) [Agent 3, PROVEN EFFICACY in subgroup analyses]. If Phase IIa enrolls predominantly liver-metastatic patients — which is the default if no stratification is applied — the trial will almost certainly fail to show a vaccine-attributable benefit regardless of STC-1010's true activity in the non-liver-metastatic subgroup.

**CrisPRO's value proposition:** CrisPRO can immediately analyze the liver metastasis status of the six patients reported at AACR 2026 (data available under NDA), model the expected impact of liver metastasis prevalence on Phase IIa power, and deliver a protocol amendment proposal that adds liver metastasis burden as a stratification factor and pre-specifies the non-liver-metastatic subgroup as the primary analysis population. This is a clinically accessible, standard-of-care data point (CT/MRI staging) that Brenus already has for all enrolled patients — it requires no new assays, only systematic recording and analysis.

## 4. CRITICAL RISKS TO CRISPRO

### Risk 1: Association with Unsubstantiated Efficacy Claims

The CEO's January 2026 statement that the program is showing "trends in efficacy that are stronger than anything that has been previously reported in hard-to-treat solid tumors" [Agent 1, VERIFIED — Claim #2] is promotional language with no comparator data, no peer review, and no statistical analysis. The 100% DCR in six patients is statistically indistinguishable from the expected activity of mFOLFOX6 ± bevacizumab alone (~80–90% DCR in first-line mCRC) [Agent 3, PROVEN EFFICACY]. If CrisPRO's name appears in Brenus press releases, investor materials, or conference presentations alongside these claims — even as a "computational partner" — CrisPRO's scientific credibility will be associated with unsubstantiated efficacy claims. This risk is particularly acute given that Brenus has a pattern of superlative language in press releases (e.g., "breakthrough," "widest panel of therapeutic targets currently available") that is not supported by the underlying data. **Mitigation:** Any engagement agreement must include explicit language prohibiting Brenus from citing CrisPRO's involvement in any public communication without CrisPRO's prior written approval of the specific language.

### Risk 2: Engagement Failure if Brenus Does Not Share Data Under NDA

CrisPRO's entire value proposition in this engagement depends on access to patient-level data that Brenus has not disclosed publicly: liver metastasis status of enrolled patients, KRAS mutation subtypes, exploratory biomarker data (cytokine profiles, immunophenotyping, ctDNA, TLS), and the identity of the human CRC cell lines used in STC-1010 [Agent 1, Section 6.1; Agent 2, Section 9]. If Brenus is unwilling to share this data under NDA — either because of IP concerns about the cell line identity, regulatory concerns about pre-publication data sharing, or simply because the data does not yet exist in analyzable form — the engagement will stall at the discovery phase. CrisPRO will have invested business development resources with no deliverable. **Mitigation:** The Phase 1 (Discovery) engagement must include a clear data-sharing commitment as a condition of proceeding to Phase 2. If Brenus cannot commit to sharing the Phase I patient-level biomarker dataset within 60 days of NDA execution, the engagement should not proceed.

### Risk 3: Trial Failure Regardless of CrisPRO's Contribution

Even with optimal biomarker stratification, adaptive enrichment design, and combination timing optimization, BreAK CRC-001 may fail. The MSS CRC IO failure landscape is extensive and consistent: GVAX + pembrolizumab (ORR 0%), AdCEA + avelumab (PFS HR 1.061, 95% CI 0.380–2.966, p=0.91 — TERMINATED for futility ⚠️ [CORRECTED 2026-04-29]), CheckMate 9X8 nivolumab+mFOLFOX6+bev (HR 0.81, p=0.30 — primary endpoint not met ⚠️ [ADDED 2026-04-29 — Weaponization Agent EQ-01]), GRANITE (primary endpoint not met), and every checkpoint inhibitor combination tested in unselected MSS CRC [Agent 3, PROVEN EFFICACY]. STC-1010's mechanism — haptenated whole-cell vaccine generating broad polyclonal immunity — is mechanistically distinct from prior approaches, but the fundamental challenge of T-cell trafficking and effector function within the MSS CRC TME has not been solved by any prior approach [Agent 3, Synthesis Memo Lesson 3b]. If the trial fails, CrisPRO's contribution will be invisible to the outside world while CrisPRO's internal resources will have been consumed. **Mitigation:** Structure the engagement as a fee-for-service or milestone-based contract, not an equity or royalty arrangement. Ensure that CrisPRO's deliverables are contractually defined and compensated regardless of trial outcome. Publish CrisPRO's methodological contributions independently if the trial fails.

### Risk 4: The n=6 Data Being Overinterpreted in Partner-Facing Materials

The AACR 2026 data (n=6, no DLTs, 100% DCR, DTH responses) [Agent 1, PRELIMINARY — n=6 only] is the only clinical data Brenus has. There is a real risk that Brenus — or CrisPRO, in partner-facing materials — will present this data as evidence of vaccine efficacy rather than as preliminary safety and pharmacodynamic signals. The 100% DCR is not distinguishable from SOC effect with n=6 and no control arm [Agent 2, ANALYTICAL INTERPRETATION]. The DTH responses are a surrogate marker of peripheral immune activation, not evidence of tumor-directed immunity [Agent 2, VERIFIED]. If CrisPRO's outreach email or any subsequent partner-facing materials overstate this data, CrisPRO will be associated with the same credibility problem that afflicts Brenus's own communications. **Mitigation:** All CrisPRO-authored materials must use the evidence level tags from the Agent evidence wall. The outreach email (Document B) has been drafted with explicit calibration of the n=6 data. Any subsequent materials must be reviewed against the seven guardrails in Agent 5 Section 5 before distribution.

## 5. GO/NO-GO RECOMMENDATION WITH CONDITIONS

### Recommendation: CONDITIONAL GO

CrisPRO should proceed to a Phase 1 (Discovery) engagement with Brenus Pharma, subject to the following conditions being met before committing Phase 2 resources. The scientific opportunity is genuine, the timing is optimal, and CrisPRO's capabilities map directly onto the program's most urgent needs. However, the engagement must be structured to protect CrisPRO's scientific credibility and commercial interests against the specific risks identified above.

### Conditions Derived from Agent 5 Gap Analysis

**Condition 1 (Non-negotiable):** Execution of a mutual NDA/CDA within 30 days of initial contact, covering the Phase I patient-level dataset, the human CRC cell line identities, and the exploratory biomarker data. Without this, no Phase 2 engagement is possible.

**Condition 2 (Non-negotiable):** Brenus must commit, in writing, to sharing the following data within 60 days of NDA execution: (a) liver metastasis status (number of lesions, % liver involvement, extrahepatic disease) for all enrolled patients; (b) KRAS mutation subtype (G12C, G12D, G12V, G13D, etc.) for all enrolled patients; (c) the exploratory biomarker dataset from Phase I patients (cytokine profiles, immunophenotyping, PBMC markers, ctDNA, HLA expression, TLS data) with the assay platforms and raw data files. If this data does not yet exist in analyzable form, Brenus must commit to a specific timeline for data availability.

**Condition 3 (Non-negotiable):** Any public communication by Brenus that references CrisPRO's involvement must receive CrisPRO's prior written approval of the specific language. This condition must be embedded in the engagement agreement, not merely agreed verbally.

**Condition 4 (Strongly preferred):** Brenus must be willing to consider a Phase IIa protocol amendment that adds liver metastasis burden as a stratification factor and pre-specifies the non-liver-metastatic subgroup as the primary analysis population. If Brenus is categorically unwilling to amend the Phase IIa protocol, CrisPRO's trial design intelligence deliverable has no pathway to implementation, and the engagement value is substantially reduced.

**Condition 5 (Preferred):** The identity of the human CRC cell lines used in STC-1010 must be disclosed under NDA to enable computational antigen mapping. If Brenus refuses to disclose cell line identity even under NDA, CrisPRO's biomarker stratification work will be limited to TME-level analysis and cannot include antigen-specific T-cell response modeling.

**Condition 6 (Preferred):** Brenus must agree to submit the Phase Ia safety and exploratory efficacy data to a peer-reviewed journal before or concurrent with Phase IIa opening. CrisPRO's analytical contributions will have greater scientific credibility if they are embedded in a peer-reviewed publication rather than a press release.

## 6. PROPOSED ENGAGEMENT STRUCTURE

### Phase 1 — Discovery (Weeks 1–8, No Resource Commitment Beyond BD)

**Objective:** Establish the factual basis for a scoped engagement and assess Brenus's willingness to meet the conditions above.

**CrisPRO actions:**
- Initial outreach email (Document B) to Paul Bravetti (CEO) and François Ghiringhelli (SAB/Study Coordinator)
- 30-minute introductory scientific call to assess Brenus's openness to data sharing and protocol amendment
- NDA/CDA execution
- 2-hour scientific deep-dive call with Brenus CSO (Benoit Pinteur) and Clinical Development Lead (Corinne Tortorelli) to review the Phase I dataset, assay platforms, and Phase IIa design plans
- Internal CrisPRO assessment of the Phase I data (under NDA) to confirm that the biomarker dataset is sufficient for analysis

**Decision gate:** At the end of Phase 1, CrisPRO's scientific leadership reviews the Phase I data and makes a binary decision: proceed to Phase 2 (scoped engagement) or terminate. The decision criteria are: (a) liver metastasis data is available and analyzable; (b) exploratory biomarker data is of sufficient quality for multi-omic analysis; (c) Brenus is willing to consider Phase IIa protocol amendment; (d) cell line identity is disclosed or a credible alternative antigen dataset is provided.

### Phase 2 — Scoped Engagement (Months 2–6, Defined Deliverables)

**Objective:** Deliver three specific, contractually defined outputs that address the program's most urgent needs.

**Deliverable 1:** Liver Metastasis Stratification Analysis — analysis of liver metastasis status in all enrolled Phase I patients; quantitative model of the expected impact of liver metastasis prevalence on Phase IIa power; protocol amendment proposal for liver metastasis stratification in Phase IIa. *Data required from Brenus:* Liver metastasis status (CT/MRI staging data) for all enrolled patients.

**Deliverable 2:** Phase I Biomarker Analysis Report — pre-specified multi-omic analysis of the Phase I exploratory biomarker dataset; ranked biomarker panel with clinical accessibility classification; composite enrichment score for Phase IIa patient selection; pre-specified biomarker analysis plan for Phase IIa protocol amendment. *Data required from Brenus:* Complete Phase I exploratory biomarker dataset (cytokine profiles, immunophenotyping, PBMC markers, ctDNA, HLA expression, TLS data) with assay platforms and raw data files.

**Deliverable 3:** Phase IIa Adaptive Enrichment Design Proposal — adaptive enrichment design options for Phase IIa; power calculations for biomarker-enriched subgroup analyses; ctDNA-based early stopping rule; historical control benchmark analysis using individual patient data from published 1L MSS mCRC trials. *Data required from Brenus:* Current Phase IIa protocol draft; Phase I patient-level clinical data (RECIST assessments, PFS events, OS events).

**Compensation structure:** Fee-for-service, milestone-based. Each deliverable is compensated independently. CrisPRO retains the right to publish methodological contributions (with Brenus co-authorship) regardless of trial outcome.

### Phase 3 — Full Partnership (Months 6–36, Contingent on Phase 2 Success)

**Objective:** Embed CrisPRO as the computational precision oncology partner for the full BreAK CRC-001 program through Phase IIa completion and Phase III design.

**Scope:** TME profiling of Phase IIa biopsies (standardized assay panel, pre-specified analysis plan, TME atlas); resistance mechanism mapping from on-treatment and progression biopsies; regulatory biomarker qualification roadmap for top 3 candidate biomarkers; Phase III design intelligence (biomarker-enriched randomized design, CDx co-development strategy); combination therapy optimization for potential Phase III combinations (TKI + STC-1010, TGF-β inhibitor + STC-1010).

**Compensation structure:** Multi-year service agreement with milestone payments tied to Phase IIa data readout, Phase III design completion, and regulatory submission. Equity or royalty component negotiable if Phase IIa data is strongly positive in the biomarker-enriched subgroup.

**Condition for Phase 3:** Phase IIa must show a pharmacodynamic signal (ctDNA clearance, DTH response, or TLS evolution) in the biomarker-enriched subgroup that is mechanistically attributable to STC-1010 and not to FOLFOX alone. If Phase IIa shows no pharmacodynamic signal in any subgroup, CrisPRO should not commit to Phase III support.

## 7. KEY MESSAGES FOR BRENUS OUTREACH

The following four messages capture CrisPRO's core value proposition for Brenus, written for a CEO/CSO audience. These are the basis for Document B.

- **"The MSS CRC IO failure landscape is not a failure of the immune system — it is a failure of patient selection."** Every prior MSS CRC IO trial that enrolled unselected patients failed. The trials that showed signals (REGONIVO, RIN, CO.26, AtezoTRIBE) all found them in biomarker-defined subgroups. CrisPRO's platform is built to find those subgroups before Phase IIa, not after Phase III.

- **"The Phase I biomarker data you are collecting right now is the most valuable dataset in your program — but only if it is analyzed with a pre-specified framework before Phase IIa opens."** Post-hoc biomarker analysis is not credible to regulators. CrisPRO can deliver a pre-specified, regulatory-grade biomarker analysis plan for the Phase I dataset in eight weeks.

- **"Liver metastasis status is the single most important variable you are not currently tracking as a stratification factor."** The published literature is unambiguous: non-liver-metastatic MSS CRC patients respond to IO at rates of 22–40%; liver-metastatic patients respond at rates of 0–5%. If Phase IIa enrolls predominantly liver-metastatic patients — which is the default without stratification — the trial will fail regardless of STC-1010's true activity. CrisPRO can model this risk and deliver a protocol amendment proposal in four weeks.

- **"CrisPRO's goal is not to validate STC-1010 — it is to give the trial the best possible chance of detecting a real signal if one exists."** We are not a cheerleader for the program. We are a scientific partner whose value is in honest, rigorous analysis. If the Phase I data suggests the program is unlikely to succeed in any subgroup, we will tell you that too.
