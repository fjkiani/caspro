Dear Paul,

I am writing following the AACR 2026 presentation of the first-in-human STC-1010 data (Poster CT051). The early safety profile from the first six patients — no dose-limiting toxicities and evidence of delayed-type hypersensitivity responses — is a meaningful pharmacodynamic signal that the vaccine is engaging the immune system as intended. I wanted to reach out directly because we have been working on a specific computational problem that I think is directly relevant to where BreAK CRC-001 goes next.

The challenge you are navigating in MSS CRC is one we have studied closely. The accumulated IO trial history in this disease is not a story of immune system failure — it is a story of patient selection failure. The trials that have shown meaningful signals in MSS CRC (REGONIVO, RIN, the CO.26 pTMB subgroup, the AtezoTRIBE Immunoscore-IC analysis) have all found them in specific, definable subgroups: patients without active liver metastases, patients with pre-existing immune infiltration, patients with high plasma TMB. The trials that enrolled unselected MSS CRC populations — including the most recent GRANITE neoantigen vaccine trial — have consistently failed to detect a signal, not because the biology was wrong, but because the responding subgroup was diluted by the non-responding majority.

The specific question I would like to discuss with you is this: **what is the liver metastasis status of the patients enrolled in Phase I, and is this being tracked as a stratification variable for Phase IIa?** The published literature is consistent that active liver metastases suppress systemic anti-tumor immune responses through mechanisms that are independent of the vaccine's mechanism of action — and that non-liver-metastatic MSS CRC patients respond to IO approaches at rates that are meaningfully higher than the liver-metastatic population. This is a standard-of-care staging variable that your sites already have for every enrolled patient. How it is handled in the Phase IIa design will have a significant impact on the trial's ability to detect a vaccine-attributable signal.

CrisPRO's computational platform is designed for exactly this kind of problem: integrating the exploratory biomarker data being collected in Phase I (cytokine profiles, immunophenotyping, ctDNA dynamics, TLS evolution) with the published MSS CRC biomarker literature to identify the patient subgroup most likely to benefit from STC-1010, and translating that into a pre-specified analysis plan that can be embedded in the Phase IIa protocol before enrollment opens.

I would welcome a 30-minute call with you and Prof. Ghiringhelli to discuss the Phase IIa design questions and whether there is a basis for a more structured scientific exchange. I am available any week in May and happy to work around your schedules.
