## THE FIVE THINGS THAT MATTER MOST

### 1. The §83(b) Election — You Have Until July 28, 2026

This is the most time-sensitive issue in the entire package.

When you receive restricted stock, you have two choices for how it gets taxed:
- **File a §83(b) election within 30 days of the grant date:** You pay tax now, on the grant-date value (which is very low — essentially par value). All future appreciation is taxed as capital gains when you sell.
- **Don't file:** You pay ordinary income tax at each vesting event, on the value of the shares at that time. If CrisPRO is worth $50M when your first shares vest, you owe ordinary income tax on your share of that $50M — in cash, even though you haven't sold anything.

**The problem:** The RSPA (the stock agreement) has two blanks that are not filled in — the share count and the par value payment amount. You cannot file a §83(b) election without knowing the exact share count and the grant-date fair market value. **The 30-day window runs from June 28, 2026. The deadline is July 28, 2026.**

**What you need to do:** Before you sign the RSPA, demand that CrisPRO: (a) complete a §409A valuation or Board FMV determination; (b) fill in the share count; (c) fill in the par value payment; (d) attach the Capitalization Certificate. Then file the §83(b) election immediately after signing — do not wait.

### 2. You Can Be Fired on Day 364 and Receive Nothing

The ICA gives CrisPRO the right to terminate you at any time, for any reason, with no notice, no severance, and no acceleration of unvested shares. Your equity vesting cliff is 12 months — meaning you receive zero equity until you have been engaged for a full year.

**The math:** If CrisPRO terminates you on Day 364 — one day before the cliff — you have provided 12 months of full-market-rate clinical advisory services and received zero compensation. Zero cash (the retainer is $0.00). Zero equity (all unvested shares are forfeited at par value, which is $0.0001 per share). Zero severance (explicitly prohibited in §9.4).

**This is not a hypothetical.** It is the default outcome under the contract as drafted.

**What you need to negotiate:** At minimum, ask for: (a) a 60-day written notice requirement before termination without cause; (b) pro-rata vesting of your time-vesting shares through the termination date (so if you are terminated at month 10, you get 10/48ths of your time-vesting shares, not zero); (c) a "good leaver" provision that accelerates the 12-month cliff if you are terminated without cause before the cliff date.

### 3. The Board Decides Whether You Hit Your Milestones — And You Cannot Appeal

Half of your equity (2.00% out of 4.00%) is tied to six performance milestones. The Board decides whether each milestone has been achieved. The contract says the Board's decision is "final and binding."

**The problem:** In a sole-founder company, the Board is Fahad Kiani. If you deliver a signed BD package to a pharma counterparty (Milestone M-B.1, worth 0.34%), Fahad can decline to certify it on the grounds that the "documentary evidence" is insufficient — and you have no legal recourse. You cannot challenge the decision in court (except for actual fraud, which is nearly impossible to prove). You cannot arbitrate it. You cannot appeal it.

**What you need to negotiate:** (a) A defined certification timeline — the Board must certify or deny within 30 days of your written certification request; (b) a "deemed certified" provision — if the Board fails to respond within 30 days, the milestone is deemed certified; (c) an independent arbitration right for disputed certifications.

### 4. Your Prior Work May Be Captured — Including Work You Did Before This Engagement

The CIAA (Confidentiality and Invention Assignment Agreement) contains two provisions that are unusually aggressive:

**The Reserved Field exclusive license (CIAA §8.3(b)):** Any prior work you have done in "AI-driven and computational oncology target identification, target prioritization, synthetic-lethal interaction analysis, biomarker-driven patient stratification for oncology, and pathway-driven oncology clinical-trial design" — even work you did before this engagement, on your own time, with your own resources — is subject to an exclusive license to CrisPRO. You cannot use it commercially outside CrisPRO, even after termination.

**The invention assignment (ICA §6.1 / CIAA §7.1(b)(v)):** Any invention you create that falls within the Reserved Field is automatically assigned to CrisPRO — even if you created it entirely on your own time, with your own resources, with no connection to CrisPRO's work.

**The University of Utah representation (ICA §7.1(h)):** You are representing that no inventions were created using University of Utah resources and that you hold unencumbered title to all your prior IP. **You were Chief of Transplant at the University of Utah. University of Utah Policy 7-002 gives the University rights in IP developed using University resources.** If you developed any clinical algorithms, scoring tools, or analytical methods during your U. Utah tenure, this representation may be false — which is grounds for rescission and potentially fraud claims.

**What you need to do:** Before signing the CIAA: (a) review University of Utah Policy 7-002; (b) consult with U. Utah's Office of Technology Transfer about any IP claims; (c) obtain a written clearance letter from U. Utah OTT; (d) complete Schedule E (Pre-Existing Inventions) with specificity. Do not sign §7.1(h) until you have done this.

### 5. The Full-Time and Co-Founder Path Is Not a Contract — It Is a Promise

The Path to Full-Time and Co-Founder Status Roadmap (Document 7) describes a path to 8% equity (full-time) and 12% equity (co-founder). It looks like a contract. It is not.

The document explicitly states it is "non-binding" and that the Board retains "absolute discretion" to decline certification even if you meet all the criteria. You could deliver everything — the BRIEF-1 licensing deal, IST enrollment, the Series A pitch package — and the Board could still decline to certify Full-Time or Co-Founder status. You would have no legal recourse.

Similarly, the Pre-Series A Investment Rights Memo (Document 9) — which describes your right to co-invest in future financings — is explicitly non-binding. You have no contractual right to maintain your equity position against dilution.

**What you need to negotiate:** At minimum, a "deemed certified" provision for the Full-Time and Co-Founder milestones, and a binding (not non-binding) pro-rata participation right in future financings.

## WHAT CRISPRO IS NOT PROTECTING (GAPS FROM THE COMPANY'S PERSPECTIVE)

The audit also identified gaps that expose CrisPRO to risk:

1. **Blank fields throughout** — The contract authority threshold, time commitment, share count, par value, CEO name, and company email are all blank. These must be filled in before execution or the documents are not enforceable.

2. **Schedule 10-C (Pre-Engagement Relationships) is not attached** — If you don't list your pre-existing relationships with academic medical centers, pharma companies, and investors, the non-solicitation provisions become ambiguous and potentially unenforceable.

3. **No independent-contractor classification protection in the time commitment** — The bracketed time commitment placeholder weakens CrisPRO's IC classification record under the DOL Economic Reality test. If the IRS reclassifies you as an employee, CrisPRO bears the payroll tax liability (and you bear the indemnification obligation under §1.5).

4. **The Board Written Consent (Document 10) is not independently verifiable** — If the Board Consent is defective, the entire equity grant has no valid corporate authorization. CrisPRO should ensure this document is properly executed before or concurrently with the ICA.

## WHAT IS WELL-DRAFTED (GIVE CREDIT WHERE DUE)

Not everything in this package is unfavorable to you:

- **The non-compete is appropriately scoped** — 6 months post-engagement, BRIEF programs only, US only. Utah Code §34-51-201 conformity is correctly cited. The garden-leave extension mechanism is fair.
- **The DTSA whistleblower notice is properly included** — This protects you if you need to report a violation to a government agency.
- **The IC classification protections are well-drafted** — Schedule A §§4.1–4.3 correctly reject employee-style time-tracking and supervision, which protects both you and CrisPRO.
- **The ROFR and drag-along mechanics are standard** — The Right of First Refusal and drag-along provisions are consistent with standard Delaware restricted stock mechanics.
- **The milestone definitions are specific** — The six milestones in Schedule B are well-defined with clear documentary evidence requirements. This is better than vague milestone language.
