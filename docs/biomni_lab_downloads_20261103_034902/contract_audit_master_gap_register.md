## STREAM A — STRUCTURAL COMPLETENESS

### A-01 | CRITICAL | Contract Authority Threshold — Blank Placeholder
**Contract:** ICA §1.6(b)(i)
**Gap:** The dollar threshold governing Kim's authority to bind the Company is a bracketed placeholder: `[$25,000]` — never filled in.
**Risk (CrisPRO):** Any contract Kim signs could be challenged as unauthorized, exposing the Company to third-party claims of unauthorized agency.

### A-05 | HIGH | Pre-Existing Inventions Schedule — Blank; University of Utah IP Trap
**Contract:** ICA Schedule E / CIAA Schedule A
**Gap:** Pre-Existing Inventions schedules are blank — no entries. ICA §7.1(h) warns that failure to list a Past Invention on Schedule E "shall not be cured by a post-Effective-Date amendment" if the institution had a pending claim Kim knew about.
**Risk (Kim):** Kim's prior work at the University of Utah (Chief of Transplant) almost certainly generated IP subject to University of Utah Policy 7-002. This is a trap that could expose Kim to breach of contract claims from both CrisPRO and the University.
**Risk (CrisPRO):** If the University of Utah asserts a claim over IP Kim assigned to CrisPRO, CrisPRO's title to that IP is clouded.
**Fix:** Kim must complete Schedule E before signing. At minimum: list any clinical algorithms, scoring tools, or analytical methods developed at U. Utah. Obtain a written non-exclusive-rights letter from U. Utah OTT before execution.

## STREAM B — IP & CONFIDENTIALITY

### B-01 | CRITICAL | Reserved Field Exclusive License — Captures Kim's Entire Prior Computational Oncology Work
**Contract:** CIAA §8.3(b)
**Gap:** Pre-Existing Inventions that fall within the "Reserved Field" (AI-driven oncology target ID, synthetic-lethal analysis, biomarker stratification, pathway-driven trial design) are subject to an EXCLUSIVE license to CrisPRO — even as to Kim himself. Kim covenants not to use, license, transfer, commercialize, or otherwise exploit such Pre-Existing Inventions outside the Company in any field that overlaps with the Reserved Field.
**Risk (Kim):** This is the most aggressive IP provision in the bundle. The Reserved Field is defined extremely broadly and could capture Kim's entire prior clinical-informatics work. Kim is permanently locked out of using his own prior work commercially outside CrisPRO — even after termination.
**Fix:** Kim must: (1) audit all prior work for Reserved Field overlap BEFORE signing; (2) list any Reserved Field pre-existing work on Schedule A with specificity; (3) negotiate a carve-out for pre-existing work that predates the engagement; (4) negotiate a sunset on the exclusive license (e.g., terminates if CrisPRO fails to commercialize within 5 years or if the engagement terminates without a Series A close).

---

### B-02 | CRITICAL | Invention Assignment — No Carve-Out for Independent Work in Reserved Field
**Contract:** ICA §6.1 / CIAA §7.1(b)(v)
**Gap:** Any Invention that "falls within the Reserved Field... without regard to whether the Recipient made use of Confidential Information or Company resources" is automatically assigned to CrisPRO.
**Risk (Kim):** Kim could develop a clinical algorithm entirely on his own time, using his own resources, with no connection to CrisPRO's work — and if it falls within the Reserved Field, it is automatically assigned to CrisPRO. This is an overreach that courts in many jurisdictions would void, but Delaware law is more permissive.
**Fix:** Add a carve-out: "Notwithstanding the foregoing, Inventions that Consultant develops entirely on Consultant's own time, without use of Company resources or Confidential Information, and that do not relate to the Company's then-current business or reasonably anticipated business, are excluded from this assignment." This is standard in well-drafted contractor agreements.

---

### B-03 | HIGH | Past IP Assignment — Pre-Engagement Informal Work Captured
**Contract:** ICA §6.2
**Gap:** All pre-Effective-Date IP "that relate to or arise from any consulting, advisory, or other work performed by Consultant for the Company or its predecessors prior to the Effective Date" is assigned to CrisPRO. Schedule E is blank.
**Risk (Kim):** If Kim did any informal advisory work for CrisPRO before June 28, 2026 (conversations, email exchanges, informal analysis), any IP arising from that work is now assigned to CrisPRO. Kim may not know what counts as "work performed for the Company" in an informal pre-engagement context.
**Fix:** Kim should: (1) document exactly what pre-engagement work he did for CrisPRO; (2) list any IP arising from that work on Schedule E; (3) negotiate a definition of "work performed for the Company" that requires a formal engagement or written agreement, not informal conversations.

---

### B-04 | HIGH | Trade Secret Enumeration — Kim Has No Reciprocal Protection
**Contract:** CIAA §1.2
**Gap:** The CIAA enumerates specific CrisPRO trade secrets with extraordinary specificity (BRIEF program delta values, FDR values, specific pharma and academic counterparty names). Kim is now on notice of all of these as trade secrets. Kim has no reciprocal protection if CrisPRO misuses his clinical expertise or relationships.
**Risk (Kim):** By signing the CIAA, Kim acknowledges these specific values and relationships as trade secrets. Any discussion of CrisPRO's pipeline — even in a general clinical-advisory context — risks a DTSA misappropriation claim (2x actual damages + attorney's fees).
**Fix:** Add a reciprocal provision: CrisPRO agrees to hold Kim's pre-existing clinical relationships (listed on Schedule 10-C) in confidence and not to use them for purposes outside the engagement without Kim's consent. Add a "residuals" clause: Kim may retain and use general knowledge, skills, and experience gained during the engagement.

---

### B-05 | HIGH | Irrevocable Power of Attorney — No Notice Requirement Post-Termination
**Contract:** ICA §6.7 / CIAA §7.7
**Gap:** Kim grants CrisPRO an irrevocable power of attorney "coupled with an interest" to execute IP assignment documents on Kim's behalf if Kim fails to cooperate. This power of attorney survives termination indefinitely.
**Risk (Kim):** After termination, CrisPRO could use this power of attorney to file patent applications, copyright registrations, or other IP filings in Kim's name — without Kim's knowledge or consent — for any Invention CrisPRO claims arose during the engagement.
**Fix:** Add: (1) a notice requirement before the power of attorney is exercised (10 business days written notice to Kim); (2) a right for Kim to cure (execute the document himself within the notice period); (3) a limitation that the power of attorney may only be used for Inventions disclosed to Kim in writing that Kim has not disputed.

---

### B-06 | HIGH | University of Utah IP Representation — Made Without Schedule E Completion
**Contract:** ICA §7.1(h)
**Gap:** Kim represents that no Inventions were created "using funding, facilities, space, equipment, or personnel of the University of Utah" and that he holds "unencumbered, sole title sufficient to assign all such Inventions." This representation is made without Kim having completed Schedule E.
**Risk (Kim):** Kim was Chief of Transplant at the University of Utah. University of Utah Policy 7-002 gives the University rights in IP developed using University resources. If Kim developed any clinical algorithms or analytical methods during his U. Utah tenure, this representation may be false — grounds for rescission and potentially fraud claims.
**Fix:** Kim must NOT sign §7.1(h) until he has: (1) reviewed University of Utah Policy 7-002; (2) consulted with U. Utah OTT about any IP claims; (3) obtained a written clearance letter from U. Utah OTT; (4) listed any U. Utah-encumbered IP on Schedule E.

### B-07 | MEDIUM | Non-Disparagement — One-Sided; No Reciprocal Obligation on CrisPRO
**Contract:** CIAA §10.3
**Gap:** Kim agrees not to make "false or disparaging" statements about CrisPRO for 2 years post-termination. CrisPRO has no reciprocal non-disparagement obligation toward Kim.
**Risk (Kim):** If CrisPRO terminates Kim and then makes disparaging statements about him to pharma counterparties, academic institutions, or investors — damaging his professional reputation — Kim has no contractual remedy. Kim's clinical credibility is his primary asset.
**Fix:** Add a mutual non-disparagement provision: CrisPRO agrees not to make false or disparaging statements about Kim to any third party for the same 2-year period.

---

### B-08 | MEDIUM | No Residuals Clause — Kim's General Clinical Methodology Is Captured
**Contract:** CIAA §2.5 / ICA §6.1
**Gap:** No "residuals" clause protecting Kim's general clinical knowledge, skills, and methodology retained in unaided memory. CrisPRO could observe Kim's clinical-advisory methodology during the engagement, replicate it internally, and then terminate Kim.
**Fix:** Add a residuals clause: "Nothing in this Agreement prevents Consultant from using general clinical knowledge, skills, experience, and methodologies retained in unaided memory, even if such knowledge was developed or refined during the engagement."

## STREAM C — PAYMENT & DELIVERABLES

### C-01 | CRITICAL | Zero Cash Retainer — Kim Has No Economic Floor
**Contract:** ICA §4.4 / Schedule A §3.1
**Gap:** Cash retainer is $0.00/month for the entire Term. Kim receives no cash compensation for any services. The only consideration is equity — subject to vesting, forfeiture, and Board certification.
**Risk (Kim):** Kim is providing full market-rate fractional CMO services (estimated $15,000–$25,000/month market rate for a physician with his credentials) with zero cash compensation. If the Company fails before any equity vests (12-month cliff), Kim receives nothing. The "Sweat-Equity Consideration" is a legal construct that benefits CrisPRO's §152(d) record, not Kim.
**Fix:** Negotiate a minimum cash floor — even a nominal $1,000–$2,000/month retainer — that triggers upon the Company's first revenue event or Series A close. Alternatively, negotiate that the 12-month cliff vests immediately upon a Sale of the Company (single-trigger acceleration).

---

### C-02 | CRITICAL | No Severance; No Acceleration; At-Will Termination — Total Forfeiture Risk
**Contract:** ICA §4.3(d) / RSPA §3.4 / ICA §9.4
**Gap:** ALL unvested shares are automatically forfeited upon any termination — including termination by the Company without cause. The Company can terminate at will (§9.1) with no notice requirement, no severance, and no acceleration of unvested shares.
**Risk (Kim):** The Company could terminate Kim on Day 364 — one day before the 12-month cliff — and Kim would receive zero equity despite 12 months of full-market-rate services. This is the most economically dangerous provision in the bundle for Kim.
**Fix:** Negotiate: (1) single-trigger acceleration of the time-vesting component upon termination without cause (at minimum, the pro-rata portion earned through the termination date); (2) a minimum notice period for termination without cause (e.g., 60 days); (3) a "good leaver" provision that accelerates the cliff upon termination without cause within the first 12 months.

---

### C-03 | CRITICAL | Milestone Certification — Board Decision Is "Final and Binding"; No Appeal
**Contract:** ICA §4.3(b) / Schedule B
**Gap:** "The Board's reasonable and good-faith determination as to whether a Performance Milestone has been achieved shall be final and binding." Kim has no appeal right, no independent verification mechanism, and no remedy if the Board declines to certify in bad faith.
**Risk (Kim):** Kim could deliver a signed BD package to a pharma counterparty (M-B.1) and the Board could decline to certify on the grounds that the "documentary evidence" is insufficient — forfeiting Kim's 0.34% allocation. The "final and binding" language eliminates Kim's ability to challenge the certification decision in court (except for actual fraud, which is nearly impossible to prove).
**Fix:** Add: (1) a defined certification timeline (Board must certify or deny within 30 days of Kim's written certification request); (2) a deemed-certified provision (if Board fails to respond within 30 days, milestone is deemed certified); (3) an independent arbitration right for disputed certifications.

---

### C-04 | HIGH | Milestone Time Windows — No Tolling for Company-Caused Delays
**Contract:** Schedule B — Milestone Time Windows
**Gap:** Each milestone has a defined time window (M-B.1: Months 1–6; M-B.3: Months 6–18; etc.). A milestone not achieved within 48 months is "treated as not achieved" and the allocation returns to the Company's equity pool. No tolling provision for delays caused by the Company.
**Risk (Kim):** Pharma BD timelines are notoriously unpredictable. A licensing deal (M-B.3) that takes 20 months instead of 18 months would forfeit Kim's 0.33% allocation — even if Kim delivered everything required and the delay was caused by CrisPRO's data package not being ready.
**Fix:** Add: (1) a force majeure / Company-delay carve-out (time windows toll if the delay is caused by the Company's failure to provide required materials); (2) Board discretion to extend time windows by up to 6 months upon Kim's written request; (3) a "substantial completion" standard.

---

### C-05 | HIGH | Expense Reimbursement — No Timeline, No Pre-Approval Process, No Dispute Mechanism
**Contract:** ICA §4.5
**Gap:** Expenses must be "pre-approved" and "reasonable." No dollar cap is specified. No reimbursement timeline is specified. No dispute process for denied expense claims is specified.
**Risk (Kim):** Kim could incur travel expenses for a pharma BD meeting and the Company could deny reimbursement post-hoc on the grounds that the expense was not "pre-approved" or was not "reasonable." With no timeline for reimbursement, the Company could delay indefinitely.
**Fix:** Add: (1) a defined reimbursement timeline (within 30 days of submission); (2) a pre-approval process with a defined response time (Company must approve or deny within 5 business days); (3) a deemed-approved provision; (4) a maximum annual reimbursement cap (e.g., $10,000/year).

---

### C-06 | HIGH | No Severance — Explicitly Prohibited Even on Termination Without Cause
**Contract:** ICA §9.4
**Gap:** Explicitly states Kim "is not entitled to any severance payment, equity acceleration, payment in lieu of unvested Restricted Shares, or any other compensation in connection with termination, regardless of the reason for termination."
**Risk (Kim):** This is an absolute prohibition on any termination protection. Combined with the at-will termination right (§9.1) and the zero cash retainer (§4.4), Kim has no economic floor. If terminated on Day 1, Kim receives nothing.
**Fix:** Negotiate a minimum termination package: (1) 30-day written notice for termination without cause; (2) pro-rata vesting of time-vesting shares through the termination date; (3) a "good leaver" acceleration of the 12-month cliff if terminated without cause before the cliff date.

---

### C-07 | MEDIUM | Full-Time Salary Caps — Significantly Below Market Rate
**Contract:** Roadmap Doc 7 — Salary Caps
**Gap:** Full-Time salary is capped at $120,000/year (Tier 1) and $180,000/year (Tier 2, conditional on Series A close). These are described as "caps, not floors."
**Risk (Kim):** Market rate for a full-time CMO at a Series A oncology AI company is $300,000–$500,000/year base salary. The $120,000 cap is approximately 25–40% of market rate. Kim would be converting from a fractional engagement to a full-time role at a significant below-market salary, with no guaranteed floor and no guaranteed bonus.
**Fix:** Negotiate: (1) a salary floor (not just a cap) at Tier 1 and Tier 2; (2) a performance bonus structure tied to milestone achievement; (3) a market-rate adjustment mechanism upon Series A close.

---

### C-08 | MEDIUM | Repurchase Price — Par Value ($0.0001/share) on Termination
**Contract:** RSPA §3.2
**Gap:** Unvested shares are repurchased at "the lesser of (a) par value paid by Purchaser per share and (b) fair market value as of the date of termination." Par value is $0.0001 per share.
**Risk (Kim):** Kim pays par value for unvested shares. If terminated, he gets back par value — effectively nothing. Combined with the zero cash retainer and no severance, Kim's economic downside is total loss of all compensation for services rendered.
**Fix:** This is standard restricted stock mechanics. The fix is at the ICA level: negotiate pro-rata vesting acceleration upon termination without cause, so fewer shares are "unvested" at termination.

---

### C-09 | MEDIUM | Pro-Rata Investment Right — Non-Binding; No Contractual Protection Against Dilution
**Contract:** Pre-Series A Investment Rights Memo (Doc 9)
**Gap:** The investment rights memo is explicitly non-binding. Kim's right to co-invest in future financings is a "posture commitment" only — not a contractual right. The Series A lead investor can override it entirely.
**Risk (Kim):** Kim could be diluted from 4% to 2% or less by a Series A financing without any right to maintain his position. Kim has no anti-dilution protection, no pro-rata right, and no contractual right to participate in future rounds.
**Fix:** Negotiate a binding (not non-binding) pro-rata participation right, even if limited to a defined dollar amount (e.g., Kim's right to invest up to $250,000 in any future priced financing at the same price as other investors). This is a standard provision for key advisors and can be structured as a simple side letter.

## STREAM D — LIABILITY & ENFORCEMENT

### D-01 | CRITICAL | Kim's Indemnification Obligation — Uncapped; Includes IP Infringement for Company-Directed Work
**Contract:** ICA §10.1
**Gap:** Kim indemnifies CrisPRO for: (a) his breach; (b) his negligence or willful misconduct; (c) any third-party claim that his Inventions or Services infringe third-party IP; (d) any tax assessed against CrisPRO on the ground that Kim should have been classified as an employee; (e) any malpractice claim from his clinical practice. The indemnification is uncapped.
**Risk (Kim):** Item (c) is the most dangerous: Kim indemnifies CrisPRO for IP infringement claims arising from his Services — even if CrisPRO directed the work. Item (d) is also dangerous: if CrisPRO's own conduct causes a misclassification finding, Kim still indemnifies CrisPRO. The carve-out ("provided that the Company has not taken any action that contradicts Consultant's independent-contractor status") is narrow and fact-intensive.
**Fix:** (1) Limit IP indemnification to Inventions Kim developed independently, not Inventions developed at CrisPRO's direction; (2) add mutual IP indemnification: CrisPRO indemnifies Kim for IP infringement claims arising from CrisPRO's own products or methodologies; (3) add a cap on Kim's indemnification obligation (e.g., capped at the fair market value of vested shares at the time of the claim).

---

### D-02 | HIGH | CrisPRO's Indemnification of Kim — "Breach" Carve-Out Is Overbroad
**Contract:** ICA §10.2
**Gap:** CrisPRO indemnifies Kim for claims arising from his performance of Services "within the scope of this Agreement and in good faith" — EXCEPT claims caused by Kim's "negligence, willful misconduct, breach of this Agreement, or violation of law." The "breach of this Agreement" carve-out is extremely broad.
**Risk (Kim):** If Kim is sued by a pharma counterparty for something he said in a BD meeting, CrisPRO could argue that Kim's statement constituted a "breach" of his representation in §7.1(g) (no false or misleading statements) — and deny indemnification. Kim would be left defending a pharma counterparty lawsuit at his own expense.
**Fix:** Narrow the carve-out: CrisPRO's indemnification obligation should only be excluded for Kim's gross negligence, willful misconduct, or intentional breach — not any breach. Add a defense obligation: CrisPRO must defend Kim (provide counsel) even if it disputes indemnification, pending resolution of the dispute.

---

### D-03 | HIGH | Pharma Counterparty Risk — Kim Named as Contact; No Direct Contractual Protection
**Contract:** ICA §10.3
**Gap:** CrisPRO commits to "customary risk-allocation protections" in pharma counterparty agreements — but this is a "commercially-reasonable best-efforts obligation, not an absolute guarantee." Kim is named as the clinical-advisory contact in pharma BD packages but has no direct contractual relationship with the pharma counterparty.
**Risk (Kim):** If a pharma counterparty sues CrisPRO for misrepresentation in a BD package, Kim could be named as a co-defendant (as the named clinical-advisory contact). Kim has no direct contractual protection from the pharma counterparty and must rely entirely on CrisPRO's indemnification — which has the broad carve-outs in §10.2.
**Fix:** Add: (1) a requirement that CrisPRO name Kim as an additional insured on its professional liability policy; (2) a requirement that pharma counterparty agreements include an explicit release of Kim in his individual capacity; (3) a right for Kim to review and approve any pharma counterparty agreement in which he is named as a contact before CrisPRO executes it.

---

### D-04 | HIGH | D&O Coverage — No Minimum Dollar Limit; Kim Not Guaranteed Coverage as Fractional CMO
**Contract:** ICA §10.4
**Gap:** CrisPRO commits to maintain D&O coverage "at the level required by the Company's corporate insurer" — but pre-Series A, "the Company is not required to maintain a specific dollar limit." Kim is explicitly told he is "not a director or officer" and his coverage under any D&O policy "is governed by the terms of that policy and is not separately committed to."
**Risk (Kim):** Kim holds the title "Fractional Chief Medical Officer" and will be named in pharma BD packages, IST agreements, and Series A pitch materials. Third parties will treat him as a senior officer. If a third party sues CrisPRO and names Kim as a defendant, Kim has no guaranteed D&O coverage.
**Fix:** Add: (1) a binding commitment that CrisPRO will maintain D&O coverage with a minimum limit (e.g., $1,000,000 per occurrence) from the Effective Date; (2) an explicit statement that Kim is covered as a "covered person" under the D&O policy in his capacity as Fractional CMO; (3) a "tail" D&O coverage commitment for at least 3 years post-termination.

---

### D-05 | HIGH | Non-Compete Scope — "Competing Activity" Definition Captures Broad ZEB1/PTEN/SPP1 Biology
**Contract:** Non-Compete §2.2
**Gap:** Kim is subject to a 6-month post-engagement non-compete (extendable to 12 months with garden-leave payment) covering all "Competing Activity" with respect to "Then-Active BRIEF Programs." The BRIEF Programs are defined at the "Target / Mechanism Axis" level — meaning a subsequent program targeting the same gene pair through a different compound is the SAME BRIEF Program.
**Risk (Kim):** The BRIEF Programs cover ZEB1, PTEN, SPP1, and NRF2 — four major oncology targets with broad clinical relevance. Kim could be prohibited from advising any company working on ZEB1-related biology (a major EMT regulator relevant to many cancer types) for 6–12 months post-termination.
**Fix:** Negotiate: (1) a narrower definition of "Competing Activity" that requires direct competition with CrisPRO's specific commercial programs, not just work on the same gene targets; (2) a carve-out for academic advisory roles (serving on a DSMB or IRB for a ZEB1-related trial is not "Competing Activity"); (3) a carve-out for clinical practice.

### D-06 | HIGH | Non-Solicitation of Investor Counterparties — Captures Kim's Personal Investor Network
**Contract:** Non-Compete §3.4
**Gap:** Kim cannot solicit any "Investor Counterparty" (any current or prospective investor of CrisPRO) for investment in any entity engaged in Competing Activity for 12 months post-termination. The definition of "Investor Counterparty" is extremely broad — any investor CrisPRO has had "material communications" with.
**Risk (Kim):** Kim has personal relationships with investors from his prior career. If CrisPRO has had any communication with those investors (even a cold email), they become "Investor Counterparties" — and Kim cannot approach them for 12 months post-termination for any competing venture.
**Fix:** Narrow the definition: "Investor Counterparty" should be limited to investors who have (a) executed an NDA with CrisPRO, (b) received a term sheet from CrisPRO, or (c) made a written investment commitment to CrisPRO. Add a carve-out for Kim's pre-existing investor relationships listed on Schedule 10-C.

### D-07 | HIGH | Drag-Along — Sole Founder Can Force Sale at Any Price
**Contract:** RSPA §5.6
**Gap:** Kim is subject to a drag-along obligation requiring him to sell his shares in a "Qualified Sale" approved by the Board and a majority of common stockholders (excluding Kim's shares). In a sole-founder company, the "majority of common stockholders excluding Kim" is effectively just Fahad Kiani.
**Risk (Kim):** Fahad could approve a sale of the Company to himself or a related party at a below-market price, drag Kim along, and Kim would be forced to sell his shares at whatever price Fahad negotiates. The "same per-share consideration" protection in §5.6(c)(i) is the only safeguard — but it only protects against disproportionate reduction, not against a low overall price.
**Fix:** Add: (1) a minimum valuation floor for any Qualified Sale that triggers the drag-along; (2) a right for Kim to obtain an independent fairness opinion before being dragged; (3) a carve-out: the drag-along cannot be triggered in a transaction in which Fahad Kiani or any affiliate is the acquiror.

### D-08 | MEDIUM | Exclusive Forum — Delaware Court of Chancery; Kim Is a Utah Resident
**Contract:** ICA §11.2
**Gap:** All disputes must be brought in Delaware Court of Chancery. Kim is a Utah resident. For a pre-Series A dispute over equity worth potentially $50,000–$200,000, the litigation cost could exceed the value of the claim.
**Fix:** Add an alternative: disputes under $250,000 may be resolved by binding arbitration (AAA Commercial Rules, arbitrator seated in Salt Lake City, Utah). Disputes over $250,000 remain in Delaware Court of Chancery.

### D-09 | MEDIUM | Non-Waivable Termination Provision — Pre-Emptively Voids Future Employment Protections
**Contract:** ICA §9.5
**Gap:** §9.5 states that any provision purporting to restrict the Company's termination authority "is null, void, and unenforceable as a matter of Delaware law," citing Gorman v. Salamone. This is used to pre-emptively void any future agreement Kim might negotiate that limits the Company's termination right.
**Risk (Kim):** This provision is drafted to prevent Kim from ever negotiating "for cause only" termination protection — even in a future employment agreement. If Kim converts to full-time employment under the Roadmap, the Company is pre-emptively arguing that any "for cause" termination protection in the employment agreement is void.
**Fix:** Add a carve-out: "§9.5 applies to this Agreement only and does not limit the parties' ability to negotiate termination-for-cause protections in any future employment agreement executed in connection with a Full-Time Conversion Event under §3.4."

### D-10 | MEDIUM | Tax Indemnification — Uncapped; Kim Bears Risk of CrisPRO's Own Conduct
**Contract:** ICA §1.5
**Gap:** Kim indemnifies CrisPRO for any tax, penalty, or assessment imposed on CrisPRO on the ground that Kim should have been classified as an employee — "provided that the Company has not taken any action that contradicts Consultant's independent-contractor status." The carve-out is fact-intensive and difficult to prove. The indemnification is uncapped.
**Fix:** Add: (1) a cap on Kim's tax indemnification obligation (e.g., capped at the fair market value of vested shares at the time of the IRS assessment); (2) a right for Kim to participate in any IRS audit or proceeding that could result in a reclassification finding; (3) a requirement that CrisPRO notify Kim promptly of any IRS inquiry and give Kim the right to control the defense of any reclassification claim.
